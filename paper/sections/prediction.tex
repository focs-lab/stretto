\section{Certifying an Alternative Matching}
\label{sec:prediction}

Fix a trace $\trace$, an alternative $\Malt$ with changed receives $\Changed$, and a
threshold $\Bt$. We first cut $\trace$ down to the part that every consistent program
repeats under $\Malt$ (the horizon), then decide $\Malt$ over that part with a
constraint graph.

\subsection{The horizon}
\label{sec:horizon}

\begin{definition}[Horizon]
The \emph{horizon} $\Hpre=\Hpre(\trace,\Malt,\Bt)$ is the greatest prefix of $\trace$
closed under the following rules. An event removed from a rank removes every later
event of that rank.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(H\arabic*)]
\item\label{H:changed} For $r\in\Changed$, the records of $\mathit{rank}(r)$ after the
  completion of $r$ are removed. Their results differ from $\trace$, so $\trace$ does not
  determine them. If $r$ does not complete in $\trace$, the records after its posting are
  removed, since a later test of its request could now succeed. The changed record of a probe
  or completion-order alternative cuts its rank after it in the same way.
\item\label{H:recv} A receive whose $\Malt$-send is not issued in $\Hpre$, or that has no
  $\Malt$-send, does not complete: the record completing it is removed.
\item\label{H:send} A send that blocks at $\Bt$ and whose $\Malt$-receive is not posted
  in $\Hpre$ does not complete: the return of a blocking send, or the record completing a
  nonblocking one, is removed. The send stays \emph{issued}, so a probe can still see it.
\item\label{H:coll} A collective whose shape requires a member that does not issue it in
  $\Hpre$ does not complete on the members that wait for it.
\item\label{H:probe} A probe whose reported send $s_p$, the observed one or for a changed probe
  the new one, is not issued in $\Hpre$ is removed.
\item\label{H:pending} A receive $r'$ posted in $\Hpre$ whose $\Malt$-send is not issued in
  $\Hpre$ stays pending. Any later receive or probe of its rank that would take or report
  a message $r'$ accepts is removed, by \ref{E:order}.
\item\label{H:ready} A ready send whose $\Malt$-receive is not posted before it in
  $\Hpre$ is removed.
\end{enumerate}
The \emph{judged} receives $\judged$ are the receives posted in $\Hpre$ whose
$\Malt$-send is issued in $\Hpre$, together with the receives posted in $\Hpre$ that
$\Malt$ leaves unmatched. $\Malt$ \emph{survives} if every $r\in\Changed$ is judged and, for a
probe or completion-order alternative, the changed record lies in $\Hpre$ and the message it now
reports or completes is issued there.
\end{definition}

Rule~\ref{H:changed} is
the only rule that refers to $\Changed$. It encodes the one fact the trace cannot
supply: what a rank does after a call returns a result the trace did not record. Every
other rule removes an operation that could not complete if everything kept stayed as
recorded. A nonblocking changed receive is judged once it is posted and its new message
is issued, because the match is decided then. Its wait may lie beyond $\Hpre$. Apart from
the cut of \ref{H:changed}, the horizon is thus an ordinary downward-closed prefix with
respect to the dependences of $\Malt$.

Rule~\ref{H:changed} is the rule of the maximal causal model, in which a thread ``is stopped
once it read a value from the memory different from the one observed
originally''~\citep{serbanuta12mcm}, and of relaxed correct reorderings~\citep{mathur18shb};
RVPredict runs on to the next branch~\citep{huang14rvpredict}. A trace of MPI calls records no
branches, so \ref{H:changed} is the branch-free instance; where the source shows a later first
read, \cref{thm:firstread} goes further. For blocking traffic, the horizon of a steal corresponds to a race
variant of reachability testing~\citep{lei06reachability}.

\subsection{The constraint graph}
\label{sec:graph}

\begin{definition}[Constraint graph]
$\Graph=\Graph(\trace,\Malt,\Bt,\Hpre)$ has a node $\issue(e)$ for every operation issued
in $\Hpre$, a node $\compl(e)$ for every completion in $\Hpre$, and a node $\mnode(r,s)$
for every judged receive $r$ with $\Malt(r)=s$. Its edges are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(G\arabic*)]
\item\label{G:po} program order on each rank, $\issue(e)\to\compl(e)\to\issue(e')$ for
  consecutive operations $e,e'$ (a nonblocking operation completes at its wait);
\item\label{G:match} for each match node, $\issue(r)\to\mnode(r,s)$,
  $\issue(s)\to\mnode(r,s)$, $\mnode(r,s)\to\compl(r)$, and $\mnode(r,s)\to\compl(s)$ if
  $\blocks_\Bt(s)$;
\item\label{G:fifo} for sends $s'$ before $s$ on one channel with $r\accepts s'$ and
  $\Malt(r)=s$: $\mnode(\Malt^{-1}(s'),s')\to\mnode(r,s)$;
\item\label{G:order} for receives $r'$ before $r$ on one rank with $r'\accepts s$ and
  $\Malt(r)=s$: $\mnode(r',\Malt(r'))\to\mnode(r,s)$;
\item\label{G:ready} for a ready send $s$ with $\Malt^{-1}(s)=r$: $\issue(r)\to\issue(s)$;
\item\label{G:coll} for each set of matching collectives, edges from issues to completions
  as their shape requires (through one hub node per set, so $O(p)$ edges);
\item\label{G:probe} for a probe that reports send $s$: $\issue(s)\to\compl(\mathit{probe})$, and
  edges into $\compl(\mathit{probe})$ from the matches \ref{G:fifo} and \ref{G:order} would
  require of a receive posted at the probe, since a probe reports what such a receive would
  take.
\end{enumerate}
A collective of a root shape without a recorded root synchronises all members.
\end{definition}

The edges order matches; three \emph{obligations} say which matches must exist.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(O\arabic*)]
\item\label{O:fifo} If $r$ is judged and $\Malt(r)=s$, each send before $s$ on its channel that
  $r$ accepts is matched by $\Malt$ to a receive posted before $r$.
\item\label{O:order} If $r$ is judged and $\Malt(r)=s$, each receive posted before $r$ that
  accepts $s$ is matched by $\Malt$.
\item\label{O:probe} If a probe completes in $\Hpre$ and reports $s_p$, each send before $s_p$
  on its channel that the probe accepts is matched by $\Malt$ to a receive posted before the
  probe, and each receive posted before the probe that accepts $s_p$ is matched by $\Malt$ to
  another send.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}[Feasibility]
$\Malt$ is \emph{feasible at $\Bt$} if it survives in $\Hpre(\trace,\Malt,\Bt)$, the
obligations \ref{O:fifo}--\ref{O:probe} hold over $\Hpre$, and
$\Graph(\trace,\Malt,\Bt,\Hpre)$ is acyclic.
\end{definition}

Match nodes are what make the order rules exact. In MPI, the constraint that an earlier
receive be matched first binds the \emph{match} of the later receive. It does not bind
that receive's completion, which for a nonblocking receive lies at a wait much later, nor
the send's completion, which for a rendezvous send follows the match. Attaching
\ref{G:order} to completions instead accepts two shapes that MPI cannot produce: a pending
nonblocking wildcard overtaken by a later receive, and a rendezvous send matched after its own
completion.

For the same reason, happens-before concurrency at a fixed point of the receive, with which
earlier tools select racing messages before
re-executing~\citep{lei06reachability,gopalakrishnan11cacm}, cannot decide an alternative.
Let rank~0 post \mpicall{Irecv($r$, ANY)}, then \mpicall{Recv(ANY)}, which takes
rank~3's message, then send $x$ to rank~2 and wait for $r$, which takes rank~1's message;
rank~2 receives $x$ and sends $s$ to rank~0. The steal $r\mapsto s$ is impossible: $r$
accepts rank~3's message, so it must be matched before the second receive, and $s$ is sent
only after that receive completes (in the graph, a cycle through \ref{G:order} and $x$). Yet
$s$ is concurrent with the wait of $r$. Placing $r$ at its completion accepts the steal;
placing it at its posting rejects it, but also every message sent causally after the
posting, which $r$ may well take. DAMPI's protocol gives $r$ its final clock at its wait or at
a later receive that must be matched after it, which rejects this steal; its theorem shows a
reported match concurrent with $r$ in that order, not realisable, and it can miss
matches~\citep{vo11thesis}. On our \nHBTraces corpus and application traces, placing receives
at their posting misses \nHBPostMissed pairs the check accepts, \nHBMGPostMissed of them NPB
MG's documented bug (RQ6), and all \nHBReplayStrettoOnly we replayed were realised by runs of
the program. Placing them at their completion selected no impossible pair there, only on this
example (idiom~14) and on \nHBSynCompOnlyNotRealised of \nHBSynPrograms random programs.

\ref{G:fifo} and \ref{G:order} need only an edge from the latest earlier receive of each
envelope class and send of each tag (\cref{app:chain}).

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]
\label{thm:sound}
If $\Malt$ is feasible at $\Bt$ with horizon $\Hpre$, then every program
$\prog\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre)$ has an execution at $\Bt$ that issues every operation of
$\Hpre$, completes every operation whose completion lies in $\Hpre$, matches every judged
receive $r$ to $\Malt(r)$, and leaves the judged receives that $\Malt$ leaves unmatched
unmatched.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} Take a topological order of $\Graph$ and read it as a
schedule. Rule \ref{E:po} holds because each rank's results in $\Hpre$ equal those of
$\trace$, except at a changed receive. Each changed receive is the last record of its
rank in $\Hpre$ by \ref{H:changed}, so consistency makes $\prog$ issue exactly the
operations of $\Hpre$. When a match node is reached, the obligations guarantee that every
earlier competitor on either side has a match, and \ref{G:fifo} and \ref{G:order} that it
has been matched already. Rule
\ref{H:pending} guarantees that no pending receive of the rank accepts the message, so
MPI's matching rule has no choice but $\Malt$. Rendezvous completions follow their
matches by \ref{G:match}, collectives follow their shapes, and probes see issued,
unmatched messages. \Cref{app:sound} gives the full proof.

\subsection{Buffering profiles}
\label{sec:buffering}

Feasibility is a function of $\Bt$, and the thresholds that matter are $0$, the distinct
message sizes of $\trace$, and $\infty$. Let $\Bobs$ be the least threshold at which
$\Mobs$ itself is feasible. If $\Bobs>0$, the recorded run cannot happen on an implementation
that sends fewer than $\Bobs$ bytes eagerly. \tool reports \cls{2-buffering} when the cycle
that shows this needs neither a probe's report nor a collective of unknown shape, so that the
recorded calls deadlock there whatever the probes see. Runtime checkers report the zero-buffer
end of this class for the observed matching~\citep{vetter00umpire,hilbrich12must}, and SMT
has decided whether a trace has a zero-buffer schedule~\citep{huang15nfm}; \tool computes the
least threshold, $\Bobs$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:obsmono}
If $\Mobs$ is feasible at $\Bt$, it is feasible at every $\Bt'>\Bt$.
\end{lemma}
\noindent The horizon of $\Mobs$ is the whole trace. Raising the threshold only removes
the edges $\mnode(r,s)\to\compl(s)$ of \ref{G:match}.

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity]
\label{thm:mono}
Let $\Malt$ be a steal at a blocking receive and $\Bobs\le\Bt\le\Bt'$. If $\Malt$ is feasible
at $\Bt$, it is feasible at $\Bt'$, and $\Hpre(\trace,\Malt,\Bt)\subseteq\Hpre(\trace,\Malt,\Bt')$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent For a steal at a nonblocking receive the horizon still grows with $\Bt$, but a receive
posted between the changed one and its wait can become judged and fail an obligation, so
feasibility can be lost as $\Bt$ grows (\cref{app:mono} gives the trace). Its feasible
thresholds still form an interval, and \tool profiles such steals at every breakpoint.

The hypothesis $\Bt\ge\Bobs$ is also necessary. Below $\Bobs$, raising the threshold can bring
back into the horizon a head-to-head exchange that is cut at the lower threshold. That
exchange then closes a cycle until the threshold exceeds its messages, so feasibility can
switch from true to false and back. \Cref{thm:mono} makes bisection over
$[\Bobs,\infty]$ exact for steals at blocking receives. \tool profiles each of them at
$\Bobs$ and $\infty$ and bisects between them. Judging every breakpoint is the
differential test. Swaps re-match two receives, which \cref{thm:mono} does not cover, so
\tool profiles them, like steals at nonblocking receives, at every breakpoint. Probe and
completion-order alternatives keep every match, and their changed record is the last of its rank
in the horizon, so nothing in the graph follows it. The rest of the graph is part of the
observed one, and their feasibility is monotone above $\Bobs$ too (\cref{lem:app-nochange});
\tool bisects their profiles.

\begin{proposition}[Deadlocks are not monotone in $\Bt$]
\label{prop:nonmono}
The set of thresholds at which a predicted deadlock occurs is neither upward- nor
downward-closed in general.
\end{proposition}
\noindent Two witnesses suffice. A head-to-head exchange of 400-byte messages deadlocks
exactly for $\Bt<400$. In MBI's loop races, a steal needs the stolen message buffered,
so the resulting deadlock exists only for $\Bt\ge 4$. A deadlock prediction therefore
names its thresholds. \Cref{prop:nonmono} is the formal counterpart, in the threshold model,
of the observation that adding buffering can add deadlocks to programs with
wildcards~\citep{vakkalanka10slack}; \cref{thm:mono} and the profiles are what is new.

\subsection{Completeness relative to the horizon}
\label{sec:complete}

Soundness alone is cheap: an analysis that accepts nothing is sound. We characterise
exactly which alternatives the check accepts, relative to the horizon.

\begin{definition}[Realisation]
Let $\Malt$ survive at $\Bt$ with horizon $\Hpre$. A \emph{realisation} of $\Malt$ over
$\Hpre$ is an execution of $\SP(\trace)$ at $\Bt$ whose events are the calls and returns of
$\Hpre$ and the matches of $\Malt$ on the judged receives, and in which each probe of $\Hpre$
reports the message $s_p$ that $\Malt$ assigns it.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness]
\label{thm:complete}
Assume that every collective of $\trace$ has a known shape and, if the shape has a root, a
recorded root, and that a ready send requires the receive it is matched to to be posted. Let
$\Malt$ survive at $\Bt$. Then $\Malt$ is feasible at $\Bt$ if and only if a realisation of
$\Malt$ over $\Hpre(\trace,\Malt,\Bt)$ exists.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof idea.} ``Only if'' is \cref{thm:sound} applied to $\SP(\trace)$. For
``if'', every obligation and every edge of $\Graph$ states an order that MPI's rules impose on
any execution with the events of $\Hpre$ and the matches of $\Malt$. A realisation therefore
meets the obligations, contains every node of $\Graph$, and orders them topologically
(\cref{app:complete}).

As $\SP(\trace)\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre)$, the two theorems say that a surviving alternative is
feasible iff some, and then every, program consistent with the trace realises it over
$\Hpre$: maximality as in the maximal causal model~\citep{serbanuta12mcm} and
RVPredict~\citep{huang14rvpredict}, for one fixed prefix.
The theorem is relative to the horizon, so it is weaker than the
optimality of SyncP~\citep{mathur21syncp}, which decides against the least closed set
containing the race and covers every sync-preserving reordering of any prefix; \tool needs the
greatest closed prefix to judge consequences (\cref{sec:levels}). A reordering of a shorter
prefix can exist while the check rejects, because the horizon keeps a receive that the
reordering omits and whose obligation fails (\cref{app:mono}). The rules
\ref{H:recv}--\ref{H:probe} and \ref{H:ready} remove only events that no such execution can
contain. Rule~\ref{H:pending} is stronger than necessary: it removes a deprived receive where
removing its completion would suffice.
The generic check meets the theorem. The fast check of steals at blocking receives works in
the observed graph, which also has events beyond the horizon, so it can reject more
(\cref{app:complete}); up to $2\cdot10^5$ records the generic check confirms each of its
rejections by a cycle, and above that the report counts the unconfirmed ones.

\subsection{Cost}
\label{sec:cost}

\begin{theorem}[Cost]
\label{thm:cost}
Let $\trace$ have $n$ records, $w$ wildcard receives of which $w_q$ on rank $q$, $\pi$ probes
that report a message, at most $T$ tags on any channel read by an \ANY-tag receive or probe,
and $\mu$ distinct message sizes, and let $C$ range over its collective sets. Then
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\roman*)]
\item there are at most $w\cdot p$ steals and at most $\sum_q \binom{w_q}{2}$ swaps;
\item one alternative is decided at one threshold in time
  $O((n+(|\Malt|+\pi)\,T)\log n+\sum_C|C|^2)$, where $\sum_C|C|^2\le p\,n$;
\item a steal at a blocking receive, or a probe or completion-order alternative, is profiled
  with $O(\log \mu)$ checks above $\Bobs$ (\cref{thm:mono,lem:app-nochange}), any other
  alternative with $\mu+2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\noindent The bound on steals is the non-overtaking kernel: only the earliest unconsumed
message of each source can be next. The horizon is a worklist fixpoint in which each record
loses its issue at most once and each rule is re-evaluated only when an input changes;
\ref{H:pending} costs one bisection, and a collective set is rescanned whenever a member is
removed ($\sum_C|C|^2$). Each pair and each probe adds a match node and at most $4+T$
class-indexed order edges, and cycle detection is linear. The check is thus quasi-linear in
$n$ per alternative and threshold for bounded ranks and tags.
Deciding whether some combination of
changed matches deadlocks is NP-complete even for single-path programs~\citep{forejt17mopper},
and placing the choice of changes between polynomial and hard cases parameterised by
$w$~\citep{shi26popl,mathur20lics,kulkarni21concur} is future work.
