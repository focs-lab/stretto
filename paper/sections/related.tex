\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Predictive analysis for shared memory, and its origin.} Feasible program
prefixes~\citep{netzer91accuracy} already excluded every event that a changed event might
control. The maximal causal model~\citep{serbanuta12mcm} made the program model explicit: a
thread is deterministic given what it reads, every program that can produce the trace is
considered, and a thread stops at its first read whose value differs; the resulting set of
runs is sound and maximal. RVPredict~\citep{huang14rvpredict} and
SeqCheck~\citep{cai21seqcheck} let such a thread run on to its next branch, and symbolic
predictive analysis keeps the recorded branch conditions~\citep{wang09ctp}. Correct
reorderings~\citep{smaragdakis12cp} keep every read's writer. WCP~\citep{kini17wcp} weakened
happens-before soundly in linear time, and SHB~\citep{mathur18shb} made it sound beyond the
first race, with relaxed correct reorderings that let a thread's last read change. M2~\citep{pavlogiannis20m2} and
SyncP~\citep{mathur21syncp} decide larger classes by closure, SyncP optimally for the
reorderings that keep the order of critical sections; SDP~\citep{genc19sdp} drops write--write
orderings, but its soundness claim was later refuted~\citep{mathur21syncp}. For deadlocks, an
SMT encoding of executability was claimed sound~\citep{kalhauge18sound}; sync-preserving
deadlock prediction runs in linear time and reports gaps in that claim~\citep{tunc23spd}.
Synchronisation-reversal races~\citep{shi24osr} reverse one lock order and test acyclicity over
a closure, the shape of our check; reversing one match is the message-passing analogue
(\cref{sec:horizon,sec:complete} relate \ref{H:changed} and \cref{thm:complete} to these
works). \Cref{thm:sound} follows the unified soundness template~\citep{liu25soundness}, with MPI's
rules at $\Bt$ for the memory model and a matching over the horizon for the bug; the template
covers locks under multicopy-atomic models, not message passing. Our witness is uniform over
the programs consistent on $\Hpre$, as in its relaxed-correct-reordering class, and
\cref{thm:l1dead} also constrains every extension of the witness. Strong trace
prefixes~\citep{ang24strong} drop critical sections that cannot affect a property; the horizon
has no such weak dependences and is an ordinary downward-closed prefix.

\paragraph{Message races and reachability testing.} Race analysis of
traces~\citep{tai97race} and reachability testing~\citep{lei06reachability} compute, from one
trace of a message-passing program, the race set of each receive: the sends not causally after
it that are the earliest unconsumed message of their source, as our steals are. A race variant
changes receives to members of their race sets and removes what a changed receive may
control, which for blocking traffic corresponds to our horizon. Each variant is asserted
feasible ``regardless of the program's control and data flow''~\citep[p.\,390]{lei06reachability}
and executed by prefix-based replay; we found no proof of the assertion. The model has no
nonblocking receives, probes, collectives or buffering threshold, and happens-before
concurrency suffices there; for MPI it does not (\cref{sec:graph}). Race detection from a trace
was extended to nonblocking operations~\citep{cypher95race}, and first and unaffected message
races are detected from a run~\citep{netzer96msgrace,park04unaffected,park07mpirace}. The MAD
environment computed the racing messages of MPI programs with the same one-per-source bound,
cut the trace at the causal future of an exchanged message, and replayed the rest with the
other wildcards pinned~\citep{kranzlmueller00thesis,kranzlmueller00mad}. \tool proves the
feasibility these works assert or test, for MPI's semantics, and predicts consequences
without executing.

\paragraph{Prediction for MPI.} Trace-based prediction of alternative matchings began with SMT
encodings for MCAPI that search the arrival orders of a trace for assertion
violations~\citep{elwakil10padtad,huang13ase}, a problem shown NP-complete~\citep{huang13ase},
and with a predictor of orphaning deadlocks from one PMPI trace~\citep{sharma12maaped}.
MOPPER~\citep{forejt14mopper,forejt17mopper} encodes deadlock detection propositionally. Its
line continues with Hermes, which records path conditions over branches on received data,
re-executes under ISP to force other matches, and models buffers of up to $\kappa$
slots~\citep{khanna18hermes}, and with SIMIAN~\citep{ranjan22simian}. Another line settles
match pairs by an abstract machine and SMT, for deadlocks, message races and zero-buffer
compatibility~\citep{huang20matchpair,huang20ase,huang23tpds}, and a third refines match sets
with logical clocks~\citep{cluster22matchsets,li24parco,internetware24}. MOPPER, the ASE 2020
analysis~\citep{huang20ase} and Hermes state soundness and completeness relative to
single-path programs, or to recorded path conditions, under fixed buffering models. None
certifies an alternative for every program consistent with the trace or computes its
threshold profile. MPI-PRV~\citep{yao26mpiprv} is sound one-trace prediction for MPI without a
solver: it carries trace equivalence and pattern-language monitoring~\citep{ang24pattern} to
MPI and checks temporal properties of real applications. It keeps the observed match of every
wildcard receive, so that control flow stays consistent, and assumes zero buffering.
Alternative matchings, which \tool predicts, are out of its scope.

\paragraph{Exhaustive exploration.} ISP~\citep{vakkalanka08isp} explores the matchings of a
program on one input, rewriting wildcard receives into specific ones during
replay~\citep{gopalakrishnan11cacm}. DAMPI~\citep{vo10dampi} takes from one run the sends not
causally after a wildcard receive, subject to non-overtaking, replays each choice, and reaches
thousands of processes by giving up completeness. MPI-SV~\citep{yu20mpisv} and
CIVL~\citep{siegel15civl} verify symbolically, and Concuerror~\citep{christakis13concuerror}
explores Erlang programs. These tools pay for every competing wildcard or give up
completeness; we re-execute only to confirm. The stress baseline of \cref{sec:eval} follows
randomised concurrency testing~\citep{wolff24rff,zhao25surw}, and state-space estimation for
DPOR~\citep{balasubramanian26dpor} estimates the exploration a program needs.

\paragraph{Complexity of message-passing consistency.} \citet{shi26popl} map the complexity
of deciding whether an abstract execution of a channel-based program is consistent, with or
without a fixed reads-from relation. Our check is their VCh-rf problem for MPI's channel
shape. In their map, hardness comes from channels with several producers and from count
capacities. MPI has neither: its queues have one producer per (source, destination,
communicator), and blocking is a per-message threshold. Given the whole matching, every
constraint is therefore a conjunction and the check is an acyclicity test, for mixed eager and
rendezvous traffic too, whereas in shared memory keeping a read's writer is a disjunction and
a race that needs one reversed critical section is W[1]-hard to predict~\citep{mathur21syncp}.
Their evaluation mutates reads-from by swapping two pairs and by redirecting a receive to an
unreceived send, the two shapes of our alternatives.

\paragraph{Buffering.} Slack elasticity holds for deterministic programs~\citep{manohar98slack}.
For MPI programs with wildcards, adding buffering can add deadlocks, and neither zero nor
infinite buffering exposes all of them~\citep{vakkalanka10slack}; Hermes reports a deadlock
that exists only with one buffer slot~\citep{khanna18hermes}. Without wildcards, a program is
deadlock-free iff it is deadlock-free with zero buffering~\citep{siegel05ppopp,siegel07europvm}.
Aislinn verifies programs under the eager and rendezvous threshold we
model~\citep{bohm16aislinn}. \Cref{prop:nonmono} is the formal counterpart of the
slack-elasticity observation; per-alternative profiles and \cref{thm:mono} are new.

\paragraph{Message passing beyond MPI.} Go channels and MPI matching are incomparable. Any
goroutine may receive from a Go channel, capacity counts messages, \texttt{select} chooses
among sends as well as receives, and channels can be closed; MPI has tags, probes,
collectives and a size threshold, and a status reveals the sender. Our results
apply to their common fragment: single-consumer channels, unbuffered or unbounded, with
\texttt{select} over receives. Trace analyses of Go report alternative communications under a
straight-line program model~\citep{sulzmann17hvc} or by vector clocks, leaving their
interpretation to the user~\citep{sulzmann18go}. AdvocateGo~\citep{advocatego} predicts
alternative partners by happens-before, rewrites the trace with the changed thread truncated,
and replays it. For Erlang-style selective receive, race variants replace a receive's message
and delete what depends on it~\citep{vidal22racevariants}, our steal and horizon without
blocking; their theorem concerns non-redundancy, not feasibility. Bita~\citep{tasharofi13bita}
reorders two receives of an actor, drops the dependent tail and forces the schedule, and
DCatch~\citep{liu17dcatch} predicts distributed concurrency bugs from correct runs and triggers
them; dl$^2$ finds communication deadlocks in deep-learning jobs observationally~\citep{gao25dl2}.
None of these proves an alternative feasible.

\paragraph{Confirmation by re-execution.} Forcing a predicted bug in a re-execution is
standard. In shared memory, deadlocks are confirmed by controlled
scheduling~\citep{bensalem06confirmation,joshi09deadlockfuzzer} and races by directed random
testing~\citep{sen08racefuzzer}; PENELOPE replays the observed prefix exactly before forcing a
violation~\citep{sorrentino10penelope}, ExceptioNULL re-executes every
prediction~\citep{farzan12exceptionull}, Portend runs both orders of a race from one
checkpoint~\citep{kasikci12portend}, and maximal causality reduction re-executes seed
interleavings that force a read to a new value~\citep{huang15mcr}. In message passing,
reachability testing replays a variant's prefix~\citep{lei06reachability}, the MAD debugger
pins the other wildcards~\citep{kranzlmueller00thesis}, ISP and DAMPI rewrite wildcard
receives into specific ones~\citep{gopalakrishnan11cacm}, MOPPER can rerun a predicted matching
under ISP~\citep{forejt17mopper}, and MPIWiz~\citep{xue09mpiwiz} and record and
replay~\citep{netzer92replay,sato15rempi} reproduce recorded sources. Specific to \tool are
pins over the whole dependency-closed horizon, taken from the certificate; a log of what each
pinned operation matched, as positive evidence of the forced match; a control replay that
must finish, so that a hang caused by an impossible pin does not count; and confirmation per
buffering threshold.

\paragraph{Runtime checkers and benchmarks.} \MUST~\citep{hilbrich12must} checks the observed
run online for argument, resource and type errors and for deadlocks. It treats standard sends
as unbuffered, and when a run stalls at a pending wildcard receive it tries that receive's
other matching sends. Version 1.10, newer than the 1.9.2 we compare with, adds checks of MPI
buffers through ThreadSanitizer~\citep{jenke23tsanfibers}. PARCOACH~\citep{saillard14parcoach}
checks collective ordering statically and dynamically. NINJA~\citep{sato17ninja} injects
network noise to make message races manifest, and reproduced the HYPRE race that we predict
(\cref{sec:eval}); our stress baseline is in its spirit. The MPI Bugs
Initiative~\citep{laurent21mbi} and \MBB~\citep{jammer24mbb} are the standard corpora; in
\MBB's own comparison the Intel Trace Analyzer and Collector scores best and \MUST has the
highest specificity~\citep{jammer24mbb}.
