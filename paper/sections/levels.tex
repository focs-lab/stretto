\section{Consequences and Levels of Evidence}
\label{sec:levels}

A feasible alternative is not yet a bug. In correct master--worker code, a wildcard with
two possible senders is the intended behaviour. \tool reports what the alternative
\emph{does}, and what the claim rests on.

\Cref{tab:classes} lists the classes. Classes \cls{1a}, \cls{1b} and \cls{1f} follow from
the changed pair alone. A count or type mismatch is an error whatever the program does
next. The consequences that happen later, a message nobody receives or a deadlock,
depend on what the changed rank does after its receive. The horizon deliberately removes
exactly that part. Each finding therefore names its assumption.

\subsection{Deadlocks inside the horizon}
\label{sec:l1dead}

Some deadlocks need no knowledge of the changed rank's continuation.

Let $\Malt$ be feasible at $\Bt$ with horizon $\Hpre$. For a rank $i$ with records beyond
$\Hpre$, let $\kappa_i$ be its first record whose return is not in $\Hpre$. A rank
\emph{terminates in $\Hpre$} if all its records, the last a finalize, are in $\Hpre$.

\begin{definition}[Contained deadlock]
A set $D$ of ranks is a \emph{contained deadlock} if every $i\in D$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(D\arabic*)]
\item\label{D:changed} $i$ is not the rank of a changed receive that completes in $\Hpre$, nor
  of the changed record of a probe or completion-order alternative;
\item\label{D:block} $\kappa_i$ blocks: it is a blocking send, receive, probe or collective,
  or a wait, not a test or an iprobe;
\item\label{D:partner} what $\kappa_i$ waits for has no partner in $\Hpre$: no unmatched send
  of $\Hpre$ that a receive or probe accepts, no unmatched receive for a send that blocks at
  $\Bt$, a required member missing for a collective (for a wait-all one blocked request
  suffices; a wait-any or wait-some counts every request);
\item\label{D:closed} every rank that could later supply such a partner is in $D$ or
  terminates in $\Hpre$, and no $\kappa_j$ with $j\in D$ is itself a partner.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Deadlock at \Lone]
\label{thm:l1dead}
Let $D$ be a contained deadlock and $\Hpre^+$ be $\Hpre$ with the calls of the records
$\kappa_i$, $i\in D$, added. For every $\prog\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre^+)$, every execution of
$\prog$ given by \cref{thm:sound} extends only to executions at $\Bt$ in which no rank of $D$
returns from $\kappa_i$.
\end{theorem}

\noindent By \ref{D:changed}, the ranks of $D$ return in $\Hpre$ the results they returned in
$\trace$, so every program consistent on $\Hpre^+$ calls $\kappa_i$ next. Consider the first
event after the execution of \cref{thm:sound} that would complete what some $\kappa_i$ waits
for. Its partner is not in $\Hpre$ by \ref{D:partner}, and by \ref{D:closed} it could only
come from a rank of $D$ after its own $\kappa_j$ returned, which has not happened
(\cref{app:l1dead}). Consistency must hold on $\Hpre^+$, not only on $\Hpre$: when the call
of $\kappa_i$ is cut, a program consistent on $\Hpre$ could issue a different call there, for
instance a send that releases another rank of $D$. A wildcard receive belongs to a contained
deadlock only if every rank of its communicator is in $D$ or terminates, because any other
rank might still send. In shared memory, deadlocked threads are
stuck~\citep{kalhauge18sound}, and sync-preserving prediction characterises deadlocks by a
closure without changing a read~\citep{tunc23spd}. Here the deadlock follows a changed match,
and an MPI deadlock is the absence of any future partner, hence \ref{D:closed}.

By \ref{D:changed}, a contained deadlock never needs the changed rank's continuation. A
blocking changed receive is the last record of its rank in $\Hpre$, so a rank that waits on
the changed rank waits on an unknown continuation, and \ref{D:closed} fails. A nonblocking
one can stop its rank \emph{before} the receive completes. Consider a nonblocking wildcard
\mpicall{Irecv(ANY)}, then \mpicall{Recv(from 1)}, then the wait. If the wildcard takes
rank~1's only message, the second receive has nothing left to take. The rank stops there,
before the wait whose result could change its behaviour. Deadlocks that need the changed
rank's continuation remain \LoneSP or \Ltwo claims. Cuts also start at the receive deprived of
the stolen message and at the released send, yet for $\Bt\ge\Bobs$ these never yield a contained
deadlock on their own. Every contained deadlock contains the rank of a receive on which $\Malt$
and $\Mobs$ differ, for a steal the rank of a nonblocking changed receive that does not complete
in $\Hpre$ (\cref{prop:app-deadrank}). Otherwise, following each blocked rank to the rank that
would have to unblock it would close a cycle in the graph of the recorded run. \tool therefore
looks for contained deadlocks only after nonblocking changed receives.

\subsection{Single-path consequences}

Most deadlocks and orphans an alternative causes do involve the changed rank. Earlier
predictors handle them by stating their guarantees for single-path programs, which issue
the same calls whatever they receive and, in MOPPER's definition, from whichever
sender~\citep{forejt17mopper,huang20ase}. In our terms, they analyse $\SP(\trace)$. The
restriction is stated, and its limits are known: an early MPI predictor named programs that
decode the status object unsupported~\citep{sharma12maaped}, racing-message testing assumed
that a race cannot change a process's future communication~\citep{kilgore97racing}, a Go
analysis models runs as straight-line code~\citep{sulzmann17hvc}, and Hermes replaces the
restriction by recorded path conditions~\citep{khanna18hermes}.
At level \LoneSP, \tool explores $\SP(\trace)$ exhaustively with the changed match forced,
using a persistent-set reduction in which only wildcard matches and \mpicall{Waitany}
choices branch. It compares the outcomes with the unforced exploration, so that a hang
the recorded program has anyway is not attributed to the alternative. Each \LoneSP
finding states whether every execution that contains the changed pair has the
consequence or only some. Its pin fixes every wildcard receive of one witness.

\begin{lemma}[Single-path is unsound]
\label{lem:sp}
There is a trace $\trace$, an alternative $\Malt$, a threshold $\Bt$ and two programs
$\prog_1,\prog_2\in\Cons(\trace)$ such that every execution of $\prog_1$ at $\Bt$
realising $\Malt$ deadlocks, while $\prog_2$ has an execution at $\Bt$ realising $\Malt$
that terminates.
\end{lemma}

\noindent The master--worker program of \cref{sec:overview} at $\Bt=0$ is the witness.
Let $\prog_2$ be the real program, which replies to \texttt{status.MPI\_SOURCE}, and let
$\prog_1=\SP(\trace)$, which replies to the recorded destination. Both are consistent
with the recorded run. Under the alternative, $\SP(\trace)$ deadlocks and the real program
does not. The lemma is narrow, and that is its point: the master depends on no payload and
takes no branch, only a destination from the status, which a definition of single path as
``does not decode messages for control flow''~\citep{huang23tpds} does not exclude. A
\LoneSP finding is therefore a claim about the recorded call sequence, not about the
program, and \tool labels it as such.

\subsection{Skeletons}

Level \Ltwo replaces $\SP(\trace)$ by a \emph{twin}: the recorded program with its
dependencies on message metadata made explicit. A destination can be computed from
\texttt{MPI\_SOURCE}. A branch can guard a collective. A payload can mirror the sender's
rank. The explorer over the twin decides \cls{1c}, \cls{1d} and \cls{1e} for programs
that agree with the twin. A twin plays the role of the branch events of
RVPredict~\citep{huang14rvpredict} and of the recorded guards of symbolic predictive
analysis~\citep{wang09ctp}, which a trace of MPI calls lacks. Twins are written by hand here;
the artifact prototypes building them by re-executing one rank on the recorded results. A
static check that a
continuation does not depend on message metadata would make \LoneSP findings sound where it
passes; static dependence analysis already relaxes value constraints in maximal causality
reduction~\citep{huang17mcrsdg}. We leave such a check to future work.

\subsection{Confirmation by replay}
\label{sec:replay}

A theorem certifies the model; the tool can still diverge from it. Every receive and
probe finding therefore carries a pin. Forcing a predicted match in a re-execution is
standard (\cref{sec:related}); what is specific here is which operations are pinned and when
a forced run counts as a confirmation. A pin names, per rank, a wildcard receive or probe by
its ordinal and the sender it must take. The replay library rewrites that operation's source
before the call, translating world ranks into its communicator. After the operation
completes, it logs the source it actually matched or reported. Pinning only the changed
receive is not enough on wildcard-heavy code: the earlier wildcards of the horizon are then
free, the replay drifts from the trace before it reaches the changed receive, and the pinned
match may become impossible. Replays therefore use \emph{horizon pins}: the changed operation
gets the alternative's sender, and every other wildcard receive and probe of the horizon gets
the sender it had in $\trace$, except the receive a steal leaves without a message, which
stays unpinned. A finding is \emph{confirmed} when three conditions hold:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item every pinned operation that completes inside the horizon matched its named sender (a
  receive whose wait lies beyond the horizon may never reach it in a run that hangs);
\item the run reaches the predicted outcome: a hang for \cls{1c} and \cls{1d}, the
  specific MPI error for \cls{1a};
\item a control replay with the same pins, but the observed sender at the changed
  operation, finishes.
\end{itemize}
Otherwise it is \emph{inconclusive}. The first and the last condition matter because
forcing an impossible match also hangs. A pinned receive can only match its pinned source,
so its completion, which the library logs, is the evidence of the forced match.
