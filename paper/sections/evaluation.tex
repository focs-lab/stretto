\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Every number below is generated from the committed result tables, which name the code commit
of each row. We ask six questions.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=*]
\item[RQ1, soundness in practice.] Does the check ever accept an alternative that no
  execution realises, and does every replayed finding behave as predicted?
\item[RQ2, effectiveness.] Which labelled bugs does \tool find from one run, compared
  with \MUST, repeated randomised runs and noise injection?
\item[RQ3, buffering.] How many recorded runs and alternatives depend on the threshold,
  and do the predicted thresholds hold on a real implementation?
\item[RQ4, levels.] Where do \Lone, \LoneSP and \Ltwo disagree, and why?
\item[RQ5, cost.] What do tracing and analysis cost?
\item[RQ6, real applications.] What does \tool report on wildcard-heavy correct code, and
  what do its alternatives change?
\end{description}

\paragraph{Setup.} Programs are built with Open MPI~4.1.6 and MPICH~4.2 on Ubuntu~24.04
and run on one node of a shared machine, in a container with 12 cores. Corpora: the
message-race, tag-matching, buffering and probe programs of the MPI Bugs
Initiative~\citep{laurent21mbi} (\nMBIprogs programs, \nMBIcorrect of them correct), and the
point-to-point and collective programs of \MBB level~2~\citep{jammer24mbb} that build with
Open MPI (\nMBBprogs; \nMBBnotBuilt partitioned-communication programs do not). \tool traces
one run of each corpus program under Open MPI and analyses it. It traces the applications under
MPICH, except AMG: Open MPI's Fortran bindings bypass the C profiling interface, and
HYPRE~2.10.1 calls MPI-1 functions that Open MPI~4 removed. The first baseline is
\MUST~1.9.2, run in \MBB's container with the same timeout, and the current release~1.11.2
in a copy of it; \MUST rebuilds each program with MPICH, so it sees a run of its own. The
second is stress testing: up to \nMBIStressRuns runs of each program with random delays of 0
to 50\,ms before the sends and receives of each rank, injected by the replay library, stopping
at the first run that does not finish. The third is NINJA~\citep{sato17ninja}, which delays
sends from a helper thread, with the same budget per setting; we repaired two defects of its v1.0.2, under
which random noise delayed no send and Fortran programs crashed at start-up.

\subsection{RQ1: soundness in practice}

\paragraph{Against exhaustive exploration.} We compare the check, in \Lone mode, with an
exhaustive explorer of $\SP(\trace)$ at $\Bt=0$ and $\Bt=\infty$, on \nEsixTraces traces: the
corpus and idiom traces small enough to explore, and random programs with synchronous and
ready sends, probes, \mpicall{Waitany} and sends after receives, of up to \nEsixMaxEvents
events. Every alternative the check judges counts, accepted or rejected. Of \nEsixPairs
(alternative, threshold) pairs, \nEsixUnsound are unsound: no accepted alternative lacks an
execution that realises its pairs on the receives of its horizon. The one incomplete pair is
a steal that does not survive, outside \cref{thm:complete}. The explorer confirms
all \nEsixLdead contained deadlocks of \cref{thm:l1dead}. With read bounds that never or
randomly allow a read, \nReadsEsixUnsound of \nReadsEsixPairs pairs are unsound and all
\nReadsEsixLdead contained deadlocks are confirmed.

\paragraph{Replays.} A finding is confirmed when a rerun with the pins of its horizon
(\cref{sec:replay}) matches every pinned operation whose completion lies in the horizon and
reaches the predicted outcome, and a control rerun with the observed senders pinned finishes.
On MBI, the findings that claim a deadlock or an unreceived send give \nMBIReplayFindings
replays, one per class and set of pins (findings of one class with the same pins share a
replay), in \nMBIReplayRuns distinct pinned runs of \nMBIReplayPrograms programs:
\nMBIReplayConfirmed confirmed, \nMBIReplayInconclusive inconclusive and
\nMBIReplayNotConfirmed not confirmed. A replay is
inconclusive when its control does not finish: \nMBIReplayInconclusiveLoop of the
\nMBIReplayInconclusive belong to racing loops labelled erroneous, which can hang without any
change, and in \nMBIReplayInconclusiveHung the recorded run itself hung. Each of them matched
its pins and reached the predicted outcome, so without the control it would have counted as a
confirmation. The \nReadsReplayFindings findings that read bounds promote to \Lone, replayed
with their witnesses' pins, give \nReadsReplayConfirmed confirmed, \nReadsReplayInconclusive
inconclusive and \nReadsReplayNotConfirmed not confirmed. On AMG, a
random sample of \nAMGsample of its alternatives, which claim no failure, was replayed: all
\nAMGsampleMatched matched their pins, \nAMGsampleFinish finished, and so did
\nAMGsampleControls controls. On Nekbone, whose late alternatives pin tens of thousands of
receives each, \nNeksampleMatched of \nNeksample matched, \nNeksampleFinish finished, and
\nNeksampleControls controls finished. The log of pinned matches never had to
overrule an outcome: of the \nPinRuns pinned runs of the applications, replays and controls,
\nPinUnmatched left a pin unmatched, and \nPinUnmatchedEnded of these hung or finished; the
others exited with an error. The buffering replays are in RQ3 and HYPRE's in RQ6.

\subsection{RQ3: buffering}

Over \nEtwoTraces traces, \nEtwoObsBuf recorded runs are not realisable at $\Bt=0$:
\nEtwoObsBufFinished finished only because the implementation buffered a send,
\nEtwoObsBufStopped did not finish anyway, and \nEtwoObsBufNever contain a ready send that no
receive matches, erroneous at every threshold. Of \nEtwoAlts feasible
alternatives, \nEtwoBdep are feasible at some thresholds only. The profiles of
\nEtwoNonmono runs or alternatives are not monotone in the threshold. We measured Open MPI's thresholds per send path with a probe program: the
shared-memory transport buffers \BimplOMPI bytes by default; with its eager limit forced down,
\mpicall{Send} and \mpicall{Isend} still send up to \BminOMPI bytes inline, while persistent
sends buffer \BpersOMPI; the TCP transport with a 64-byte limit buffers \BtcpOMPI bytes on
every path. An earlier run of the replays below forced the shared-memory limit down instead, and
two persistent-send programs hung there where the single threshold of the send path predicted
they would finish; after measuring per path, we moved the replays to TCP.

Each of the \nEtwoReplayPrograms programs whose run finished and needs a threshold is rerun
three times. With the default limits it must finish again (\nEtwoReplayDefaultOK did). On TCP,
with the threshold one byte below the predicted bound, it must hang: \nEtwoReplayBelowOK of
\nEtwoReplayBelowTestable did. The other four send to themselves, which the self transport
does inline up to 256 bytes, so no setting tests them. With the threshold at the bound, it
must finish: \nEtwoReplayAtOK of \nEtwoReplayAt did. \nEtwoReplayBad replays went against
the prediction.

\subsection{RQ4: levels}

\Lone flags a racing receive; \LoneSP adds the consequences the single path shows. On MBI,
\Lone reports \nMBIoneFfind same-shape alternatives (\cls{1f}) in \nMBIoneFprogs programs.
\LoneSP certifies \nMBIspCfind unreceived sends (\cls{1c}) in \nMBIspCprogs programs and
\nMBIspDfind deadlocks (\cls{1d}) in \nMBIspDprogs. On the idioms, \LoneSP's deadlock of the master--worker program
(idiom~01) is spurious: the real program's loop ends on the count of results, and its
\Ltwo twin finishes. The MBI races that \tool misses abort unless the payload, the sender's
rank, is~3; the single path has no branch to reach the abort, and their \Ltwo twins, written
knowing the labels, certify it (\cls{1e}) in every case. Without read bounds no corpus program has a contained deadlock
(\cref{thm:l1dead}). With the bounds computed statically from the sources, the
consequences of \nReadsMoved erroneous programs, \nReadsMovedClean with a clean run, move from
\LoneSP to \Lone, through deadlocks contained in the longer horizon or witnesses within
the bounds (\cref{thm:firstread}); \Lone alone then scores F1 \nReadsFoneLOne instead of
\nReadsFoneLOneBase, with \nReadsNewFP new false positives. The value-checking programs read
the payload first and stay at \Ltwo.

\subsection{RQ5: cost}

We timed NPB's kernels in one announced leg during which the machine ran nothing else,
\nEfourReps times each, plain and traced, on four and sixteen processes; sixteen share the
container's 12 cores through hyperthreads. On the kernels whose timed section lasts at least a second, the
traced section took \TraceOverhead times as long as the plain one, by NPB's own timer, the most
for lu.A on sixteen processes, which writes the largest trace. Shorter sections, a few
hundredths of a second, give larger and noisier ratios. In wall time, which includes the
launcher's start-up, every traced run took \TraceOverheadWall times as long.
The tracer flushes one line per call, so that a killed rank leaves a complete trace.
Analysis in one process took \LuAnalysis\,s for lu.A on sixteen processes, \LuEvents million
events, and \AMGtime\,s for AMG with its \nAMGalts probe alternatives. On Nekbone's
communication suite, \NekRecords million records, it took \NekMinutes minutes and \NekGB\,GB,
outside the timed leg, to check \nNekChecked receive alternatives, \nNekAlts of them accepted:
about \NekPerAlt\,s per check against the whole graph (\cref{thm:cost}).
Also outside it, from \nScaleNpMin to \nScaleNpMax processes (oversubscribed above 24
hardware threads), AMG's analysis grows from \ScaleAMGSixteenTime\,s and \ScaleAMGSixteenGB\,GB to
\ScaleAMGSixtyFourTime\,s and \ScaleAMGSixtyFourGB\,GB and its probe alternatives from
\nScaleAMGSixteenProbeAlts to \nScaleAMGSixtyFourProbeAlts, each costing time about linear in the
trace (\ScaleAMGSixteenPerAlt to \ScaleAMGSixtyFourPerAlt\,ms); HYPRE's grows from
\ScaleHypreSixteenTime to \ScaleHypreSixtyFourTime\,s, LU's \ScaleLuTimeRatio-fold for a
\ScaleLuRecordsRatio-fold trace. All \nScaleSample sampled replays and their controls matched
their pins and finished.

\subsection{RQ6: real applications}

\paragraph{A production bug from one clean run.} HYPRE~2.10.1's ParaSails preconditioner
exchanges matrix rows twice, first the pruned rows and then the stored rows. Both exchanges
send their requests with tag~222 and take them with \mpicall{Probe(ANY\_SOURCE, 222)}
followed by a receive from the probed source. A rank that has finished the first exchange can
send its stored-rows request while a neighbour is still probing for pruned-rows requests. The
probe then takes it, and the run hangs. A message race in HYPRE~2.10.1 hung a production code
intermittently for 18 months, and diagnosing it took 291 jobs and 10\,392 compute-node
hours~\citep{sato17ninja}. Version~2.11.0 fixed ``an MPI bug that was causing Parasails to hang
occasionally''~\citep{hypre-changelog}, by giving the two exchanges different tags.

We traced \nHypreFourRuns runs of HYPRE's \texttt{ij} driver with ParaSails-PCG on four
processes and \nHypreEightRuns on eight. Every run finished normally. In each trace we replayed
every alternative of a request probe, whose tag the documented fix changed, with the pins of its
horizon and with its control. An
alternative is \emph{cross} when the probe would take a request of the other exchange, which is
the race, and \emph{same} when it would take another neighbour's request of its own exchange.
The trace tells the two apart. An exchange sends its requests, counts the replies to expect
with an \mpicall{Allreduce}, and only then probes. A request and a probe therefore belong to
one exchange exactly when the probing rank has made one more collective call before the probe
than the sending rank before the request.

\Cref{tab:hypre} gives the outcome. On four processes, \nHypreFourExposed of the
\nHypreFourRuns traces have a cross alternative, \nHypreFourAlts in all. On eight, every trace
has one, and there are \nHypreEightAlts. Every cross alternative hangs when replayed, with its pins
matched as in RQ1, and every control finishes. The same alternatives finish with the
residual of their control: all \nHypreFourLocalAlts on four processes, and
\nHypreEightLocalSameResidual of the \nHypreEightLocalAlts on eight. On version~2.11.0, the
fix, no trace has a cross alternative, and its same alternatives finish too:
\nHypreFixedFourLocalFinish of \nHypreFixedFourLocalAlts on four processes and
\nHypreFixedEightLocalFinish of \nHypreFixedEightLocalAlts on eight. Its other
\nHypreFixedEightLocalOther replays and \nHypreFixedEightLocalControlsFailed controls, and the
remaining replay of 2.10.1, exited with status~15 without finishing; plain runs launched
the same way never did, their output was not kept, and we count them as neither hanging nor
finishing.
Stress testing never hit the hang: \nHypreStressRuns untraced runs of
version~2.10.1, half with random delays, all finished. NINJA, built for this race,
hangs it in \nNinjaHypreEightAppHangSetup of \nNinjaHypreEightAppRuns runs on eight processes
in its application-centric mode and in \nNinjaHypreEightRandomShortHangSetup of
\nNinjaHypreEightRandomShortRuns with 10\% of sends delayed by 50\,ms; on four processes only
random noise does (\nNinjaHypreFourRandomShortHangSetup of \nNinjaHypreFourRandomShortRuns),
and never on 2.11.0.

A trace has a cross alternative only if it recorded an order in which a neighbour could reach
the next exchange while this rank still probes. The run finishes either way: the order is
common, the hang it allows is not.

The claim has two parts. That the probe can report the other exchange's request is an \Lone
finding, true for every program consistent with the trace; \tool reports it as a probe
alternative, a warning, noting that a count taken from the probe would differ. That the run
then hangs is not a claim of the analysis: ParaSails receives from the probed source and
replies to it (\texttt{status.MPI\_SOURCE}), the pattern of \cref{lem:sp}, so the continuation
lies beyond the horizon. The pinned replays and their controls show the hang. The race needs
buffering: in a trace analysed before the campaign, the check certifies the cross alternative
only for $\Bt\ge\BHypreCross$ bytes, the size of the neighbour's reply in the first exchange,
which must go eagerly for the neighbour to leave that exchange while this rank still probes.
We did not measure the threshold of MPICH, under which every forced cross match happened. In a
trace on four processes the
probe alternatives fall at four call sites: HYPRE's own \mpicall{Iprobe} wrapper in its data
exchange, the diagonal-scaling exchange, ParaSails's \texttt{ReceiveReplyPrunedRows}, and its
\texttt{ReceiveRequest}. Every alternative replayed in the campaign lies at the last, the probe
for requests (\texttt{ParaSails.c:166}).

\paragraph{A second documented bug.} The halo exchange of NPB MG~2.4.1 posts every receive
with \anysrc and a tag that depends on the axis and the direction but not on the grid level.
NPB's change log records that this ``could cause MPI\_Wait return a message meant for the
wrong k'', and version~3.2.1 fixed it by receiving from the named
neighbour~\citep{npb-changelog}. We traced \nMGTraced clean runs of class~S on \nMGRanks
processes. Within one exchange each receive has its own tag and one sender, so an accepted
alternative takes a shorter message of another exchange, which MPI allows; \nMGExposed traces
have such alternatives, \nMGAlts in all. Taking that message is an \Lone finding; what follows
lies beyond the horizon, so we replayed every alternative with its horizon pins and its
control. Of the \nMGReplayed replays, \nMGMatched matched their pins,
\nMGFinished finished, and \nMGChanged computed another residual than their control.
\nMGFailRel of these fail the relative check of later NPB versions, but only \nMGFailAbs fail
the check of version~2.4.1, which accepts an absolute error of $10^{-8}$: most wrong results
pass it. \nMGControls controls finished, \nMGControlsSame of them with the residual of the
traced run. Version~3.3.1 has no alternative in its \nMGFixedTraced traces. Random delays
change the residual as well, in \nMGStressChanged of \nMGStressRuns untraced runs of
version~2.4.1 on \nMGStressRanks processes, half of them delayed, and NINJA in
\nNinjaMGSixteenRandomChanged (random noise) and \nNinjaMGSixteenAppChanged
(application-centric) of \nNinjaMGSixteenRandomRuns runs on 16.

\paragraph{A third documented bug.} deal.II~9.3.0-rc1's \texttt{RemotePointEvaluation} takes
each call's messages with \mpicall{Probe} on \anysrc under one tag, and nothing separates two
calls on one cached object; release~9.3.0 added a mutex~\citep{dealii-pr12363}. Each of
\nDealTraced clean runs of its test \texttt{mapping\_01} on \nDealRanks processes predicts
probes that take the next call's message, \nDealAlts in all. Forced, all \nDealReplayed finish
with status~0 and print wrong values in the two consecutive calls the code predicts
(\nDealMatched report every pin matched); \nDealControlsSame of \nDealControls controls print
the expected output, and 9.3.0 has no such alternative. Unlike HYPRE's, this race is easy to
hit: \nDealStressDelayChanged of \nDealStressDelayRuns runs with random delays print wrong values.

\paragraph{Wildcard-heavy correct code.} AMG's solver exchanges its matrix data with probes on
any source. One traced run on eight processes, with \nAMGsends messages, \nAMGwild wildcard
receives and \nAMGprobes probes, gives \nAMGalts probe alternatives and \nAMGrecvAlts
same-shape receive alternatives. All are warnings or information, none a
contained deadlock. RQ1's sample finished under every pin. In Nekbone, every finding is a \cls{1f} at the binary-tree reduction \texttt{gp2}, whose inner
nodes take their children's contributions with one tag from any source. Only the
communication suite's timing test calls it; the solver sums with \mpicall{Allreduce}. The
check accepts \nNekAlts alternatives there, the same four at each of the test's calls, and
rejects \nNekRejected for one reason: the
other sender's next message with that tag is sent only after the receive completes, so no
execution delivers it there. Its RQ1 sample finished under every pin too.

\subsection{Threats to validity}

All runs used one node and at most \nScaleNpMax processes, and the corpora's programs are
small; the exhaustive comparison of RQ1 reaches \nEsixMaxEvents events. Of
\cref{tab:rq2}'s runs only MBI's were repeated, without \MUST. The \Ltwo twins are
written knowing the labels, and the HYPRE campaign replays only the probe whose tag the documented fix
changed. \MUST sees its own run under MPICH, not the traced one,
and for both the harness's timeout decides what is a hang.
Stress testing and NINJA ran at the default eager limits, one setting per mode; others
or a lower limit may find more. The model assumes a
complete trace of the modelled fragment (a call outside it limits the report to observed
findings), one threshold for all standard sends of a run, and an angelic choice in
\mpicall{Waitany}. The HYPRE campaign uses one input and two process counts, MG's
one class and one count, deal.II's one test.
