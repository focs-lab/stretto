\section{Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}

\providecommand{\evcall}{\mathit{call}}
\providecommand{\evret}{\mathit{ret}}

This appendix proves the results of \cref{sec:prediction,sec:levels} as stated there. For
monotonicity (\cref{app:mono}), completeness (\cref{app:complete}) and the contained deadlock
(\cref{app:l1dead}) it also gives, for each hypothesis, a trace on which the statement fails
without it. Labels (O$n$), (F$n$), (C$n$), (D$n$), (P$n$), (H8) and (K) are local. Here $p$
denotes a probe and $|\Ranks|$ the number of ranks.

\subsection{Conventions}
\label{app:conv}

\paragraph{Events.}
Rank $i$ has records $0,\dots,n_i-1$. Record $k$ has a call $\evcall_{i,k}$ and a return
$\evret_{i,k}$ that carries its result, in the order $\evcall_{i,0}\,\evret_{i,0}\,
\evcall_{i,1}\cdots$. For an operation $e$, $\issue(e)$ is the call of the record that posts
$e$, and $\compl(e)$ is the return of the record that completes $e$ in $\trace$: its own record
if $e$ is blocking, else the wait or test that returned it; \ref{G:po} reads
$\evcall_{i,k}\to\evret_{i,k}\to\evcall_{i,k+1}$. The $k$-th collectives on a communicator
form a \emph{set}.

\paragraph{Executions.}
An execution is a finite event sequence that satisfies \ref{E:po}--\ref{E:probe}. Nothing forces
progress: an execution may stop anywhere and may leave a posted receive and an issued send it
accepts both unmatched; a test may return false and an iprobe report nothing. A receive returns
the envelope and payload of its matched send, and an operation includes its arguments. We use
two premises: (P1) a collective's result at a member depends only on the arguments of the
members its shape requires; (P2) a rank makes no call after finalising.

\paragraph{Prefixes.}
A prefix of $\trace$ (\cref{sec:model}) gives each rank a \emph{cut state} $(k,b)$: the events of
records $0,\dots,k-1$, plus $\evcall_{i,k}$ if $b=1$, ordered $(k,0)<(k,1)<(k+1,0)$. An
operation is \emph{posted} or \emph{issued} in $\Hpre$ if its call is in $\Hpre$, and
\emph{completes} in $\Hpre$ if its completion is. $\prog\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre)$ if, whenever
$\evcall_{i,k}\in\Hpre$, $\prog_i$ applied to the results of records $0,\dots,k-1$ in $\trace$
yields record $k$. A probe or completion-order alternative (\cref{sec:model}) keeps $\Malt=\Mobs$ and changes
one record $v$, the \emph{changed record}: \ref{H:changed} cuts its rank after $v$, and it
survives if $v$ is in $\Hpre$ with the message it now reports, or the send of the request it now
returns, issued in $\Hpre$. If $v$ is a completion call that now returns the request of $r_j$,
$\compl(r_j)$ is $\evret(v)$.

\paragraph{Obligations.}
For a probe $p$, $p\accepts s$ is defined as for a receive, and $s_p$ is the send $p$ reports:
in $\trace$, or under the alternative if $p$ is its changed record. We read the obligations of
\cref{sec:graph} as follows.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item[(O1)] If $r\in\judged$ and $\Malt(r)=s$, each send $s'$ before $s$ on its channel with
  $r\accepts s'$ is matched by $\Malt$ to a receive posted before $r$.
\item[(O2)] If $r\in\judged$ and $\Malt(r)=s$, each receive posted before $r$ that accepts $s$
  is matched by $\Malt$.
\item[(O3)] If a probe $p$ completes in $\Hpre$, each send $s'$ before $s_p$ on its channel
  with $p\accepts s'$ is matched by $\Malt$ to a receive posted before $p$, and each receive
  posted before $p$ that accepts $s_p$ is matched by $\Malt$ to a send other than $s_p$.
\end{itemize}
$\Malt$ is feasible if it survives, (O1)--(O3) hold and $\Graph$ is acyclic. The
implementation checks (O1) and (O2) for the judged receives and (O3) for the probes in $\Hpre$,
on the ranks whose obligations the alternative can change. The clause ``posted before $r$'' of (O1) is necessary. Without it \cref{thm:sound} fails: rank~0
runs $\mpicall{Recv}(r,\ANY)$, $\mpicall{Recv}(q,\text{from }1)$; rank~1 sends $s'$ then $s$,
rank~2 sends $s_o$; $\trace$ has $r\gets s_o$, $q\gets s'$. The alternative $r\mapsto s$ would
pass, as $s'$ is matched to $q$, but $r$ cannot take $s$ while $s'$ is pending.

\paragraph{Steals and the observed threshold.}
A steal $r\mapsto s$ is $\Malt=\Mobs[r\mapsto s]$ minus the pair $(r_2,s)$, if
$r_2=\Mobs^{-1}(s)$ exists. It \emph{releases} $s_o=\Mobs(r)$, if that exists. The steals
\tool enumerates are \emph{kernel steals}: each send before $s$ on its channel
that $r$ accepts is matched by $\Mobs$ to a receive posted before $r$, and $s$ is not; so $r_2$
is posted after $r$. $\Bobs$ is the least $\Bt$ at which $\Hpre(\trace,\Mobs,\Bt)=\trace$,
(O1)--(O3) hold for $\Mobs$ and $\Graph(\trace,\Mobs,\Bt,\trace)$ is acyclic, as in the
single-path check of the observed run, where every cut is a hang. Then (F1) $\Mobs$ meets
(O1)--(O3) on all of $\trace$, and (F2) $\hat G_{\Bt}=\Graph(\trace,\Mobs,\Bt,\trace)$ is
acyclic for $\Bt\ge\Bobs$ (\cref{lem:obsmono}). By (F1), $s_o$ is not on the channel of a
kernel steal's $s$: before $s$ it would be matched to $r$ itself, after $s$ it would violate
(O1) for $(r,s_o)$. \tool takes $\Bobs$ over every edge of $\hat G_\Bt$, as here, and does not
check (F1), which every run of an MPI library meets. Its \cls{2-buffering} finding keeps only the
edges that hold whatever the probes report and whatever a collective of unknown shape does, so it
can name a lower threshold than $\Bobs$.

\subsection{The horizon is a greatest fixpoint}
\label{app:fix}

For a prefix $X$, let $\mathit{tgt}(X)$ be the events the rules target on $X$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \ref{H:changed}: for $r\in\Changed$, the call that follows $\compl(r)$, or that follows the
  posting of $r$ if $r$ does not complete in $\trace$;
\item \ref{H:recv}: for a receive that completes in $\trace$ and whose $\Malt$-send is undefined
  or not issued in $X$, the call of its completing record;
\item \ref{H:send}: for a send that completes in $\trace$, blocks at $\Bt$, and whose
  $\Malt$-receive is undefined or not posted in $X$, its return if it is blocking, else the call
  of its completing record;
\item \ref{H:coll}: for a collective that completes in $\trace$, if a member its shape requires
  does not issue the set in $X$, the call of its completing record;
\item \ref{H:probe}: for a probe $p$ with $s_p$ not issued in $X$, the call of $p$;
\item \ref{H:pending}: for a receive $r'$ posted in $X$ whose $\Malt$-send is undefined or not
  issued in $X$, the calls of the later receives $r''$ of its rank with $r'\accepts\Malt(r'')$
  and of the later probes $p$ of its rank with $r'\accepts s_p$;
\item \ref{H:ready}: for a ready send whose $\Malt$-receive is undefined or not posted in $X$,
  its call.
\end{itemize}
\ref{H:ready} drops ``before it'': a prefix does not order ranks, and \ref{G:ready} does. Let
$F(X)$ keep, on each rank, the longest prefix of $X$ that avoids $\mathit{tgt}(X)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:app-fix}
$F$ is deflationary and monotone. Its greatest fixpoint exists, is unique and equals
$F^m(\trace)$ for all large $m$. A prefix is closed under the rules iff it is a fixpoint, so
the greatest fixpoint is the horizon. A worklist that re-evaluates a rule instance whenever one
of its input events leaves the prefix, as the implementation does, computes it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$F(X)\subseteq X$ by definition. Let $X\subseteq Y$. Then $\mathit{tgt}(Y)\cap X\subseteq \mathit{tgt}(X)$: every
condition except that of \ref{H:changed}, which has none, says that some event is not in the
prefix, and carries over from $Y$ to $X$; \ref{H:pending} also needs $r'$ posted, which holds in
$X$ because its target is a later call of the same rank. So $F(X)_i$ is a prefix of $Y_i$ that
avoids $\mathit{tgt}(Y)$, and $F(Y)_i$ is the longest one: $F(X)\subseteq F(Y)$. The prefixes form a finite
lattice, so $\trace\supseteq F(\trace)\supseteq\cdots$ stops at some $X^*=F(X^*)$. A fixpoint
$Z$ satisfies $Z=F^m(Z)\subseteq F^m(\trace)$, so $Z\subseteq X^*$, and $X^*$ is the unique
greatest fixpoint. A prefix is closed iff no rule targets an event of it, iff it is a
fixpoint.

The worklist lowers one rank at a time, from $\trace$, to a target $v$ of the current
prefix $X_t$. If $v$ were in $\Hpre\subseteq X_t$, then $v\in \mathit{tgt}(\Hpre)$, which $\Hpre=F(\Hpre)$
excludes; so $\Hpre\subseteq X_{t+1}$. The loop ends since each step removes an event. Each rule
instance fires on ``$e\notin X$'' for $e$ in a fixed set, and the worklist re-evaluates it when
such an $e$ leaves: the call of a send triggers \ref{H:recv} and \ref{H:pending} for its
$\Malt$-receive and \ref{H:probe} for its probes; the call of a receive, \ref{H:send} and
\ref{H:ready} for its $\Malt$-send; the call of a collective, \ref{H:coll} for its set. So the
result is a fixpoint containing $\Hpre$, that is, $\Hpre$.
\end{proof}

The implementation applies \ref{H:pending} only to receives that complete in $\trace$, and the
proofs below apply to its fixpoint as well: they need the rule only for those, since a changed
receive that is not judged fails survival, and (O2), (O3) and (F1) cover receives without an
$\Malt$-send.

\subsection{Class-indexed order edges}
\label{app:chain}

An \emph{envelope class} is a set of receives of one rank and communicator with the same source
field (a rank or \ANY) and tag field. Its members accept the same sends, and the receives that
accept $s$ lie in four classes. Let $G^{c}$ keep, of the order edges into the match of a judged
pair $(r,s)$ (or into $\compl(p)$ with $s_p=s$), those from the latest receive posted before $r$
(or $p$) in each of the four classes, and from the latest send of each accepted tag before $s$
on its channel. The implementation builds these edges.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:app-chain}
If $\Hpre$ is closed under \ref{H:pending} and (O1)--(O3) hold, $G^{c}$ and $\Graph$ have the
same reachability relation; one is acyclic iff the other is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$G^{c}\subseteq\Graph$; we show that each edge of $\Graph$ is a path of $G^{c}$. Take an edge
$\mnode(r'',\Malt(r''))\to\mnode(r,s)$ of \ref{G:order}, and induct on the number of receives
between $r''$ and $r$. Let $r_1$ be the latest receive of the class of $r''$ before $r$. As
$r_1\accepts s$, (O2) makes $\Malt(r_1)$ defined, and it is issued in $\Hpre$, else
\ref{H:pending} removes $r$. So $r_1\in\judged$ and $G^{c}$ has
$\mnode(r_1,\Malt(r_1))\to\mnode(r,s)$. If $r_1\ne r''$, then $r''$ precedes $r_1$ and accepts
$\Malt(r_1)$, so $\Graph$ has $\mnode(r'',\Malt(r''))\to\mnode(r_1,\Malt(r_1))$, a path of
$G^{c}$ by induction. For an edge $\mnode(q,s'')\to\mnode(r,s)$ of \ref{G:fifo}, induct on the
sends between $s''$ and $s$. Let $s_1$ be the latest send with the tag of $s''$ before $s$ on the
channel. By (O1), $q_1=\Malt^{-1}(s_1)$ is posted before $r$, so $q_1\in\judged$ and $G^{c}$ has
$\mnode(q_1,s_1)\to\mnode(r,s)$. If $s_1\ne s''$, then $q_1\accepts s''$, so $\Graph$ has
$\mnode(q,s'')\to\mnode(q_1,s_1)$. Edges of \ref{G:probe} reduce to these cases, with $p$ for
$r$ and (O3) for (O1) and (O2).
\end{proof}

The same chains let the implementation decide (O1) and (O2) from the latest send of each tag and
the earliest unmatched receive of each class. Merging the match node of a blocking receive with
its return, and the hub nodes of \ref{G:coll}, also keep reachability between the remaining
nodes.

\subsection{Soundness}
\label{app:sound}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:sound}]
Let $\Malt$ be feasible at $\Bt$ with horizon $\Hpre$, and $\prog\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre)$. Read a
topological order of $\Graph$ as a sequence $X$ of the calls and returns of $\Hpre$ and the
matches $\mnode(r,\Malt(r))$, $r\in\judged$. Each return carries its result in $\trace$,
except that the return completing $r\in\Changed$ carries the envelope and payload of
$\Malt(r)$, and a changed record returns its new report or request. We show that $X$ is an
execution of $\prog$ at $\Bt$; by \cref{lem:app-chain} this also covers the implementation's
graph.

\emph{\ref{E:po} and results}, by induction along $X$. A call $\evcall_{i,k}$ follows
$\evret_{i,k-1}$ (\ref{G:po}). No earlier record of rank $i$ is changed or completes a changed
receive, since \ref{H:changed} removes all of the rank after one, so records $0,\dots,k-1$
returned their results in $\trace$, and consistency makes $\prog_i$ yield record $k$. The library
may return each result: a receive $r'\notin\Changed$ is matched to $\Malt(r')=\Mobs(r')$, whose
call has its arguments in $\trace$; a probe reports $s_p$ (see \ref{E:probe}); a wait or test
returns the requests it returned in $\trace$, which complete there, and a false test stays false;
a changed completion call returns the request of $r_j$, which is judged by survival and whose
match \ref{G:match} puts before $\compl(r_j)=\evret(v)$; a collective's result follows from (P1),
as the required calls precede it.

\emph{\ref{E:match}.} Matches are pairs of the injective $\Malt$, after both calls
(\ref{G:match}).

\emph{\ref{E:fifo}.} Let $\mnode(r,s)\in X$, and let $s'$ precede $s$ on its channel with
$r\accepts s'$. By (O1), $q=\Malt^{-1}(s')$ is posted before $r$, and $s'$ is issued as $s$ is.
So $q\in\judged$, and \ref{G:fifo} puts $\mnode(q,s')$ first.

\emph{\ref{E:order}.} Let $r'$ be posted before $r$ with $r'\accepts s$. By (O2), $\Malt(r')$ is
defined; it is issued in $\Hpre$, else \ref{H:pending} removes $r$. So $r'\in\judged$, and
\ref{G:order} puts its match first.

\emph{\ref{E:compl}.} If $r'$ completes in $\Hpre$, \ref{H:recv} makes $\Malt(r')$ issued, and
\ref{G:match} puts the match before $\compl(r')$. If a send $s'$ that blocks at $\Bt$ completes,
\ref{H:send} makes $\Malt^{-1}(s')$ posted, and \ref{G:match} puts the match before $\compl(s')$.
For a ready send $s'$, \ref{H:ready} makes $q=\Malt^{-1}(s')$ posted, \ref{G:ready} puts
$\issue(q)$ before $\issue(s')$, and $q$ is unmatched then, as its match follows $\issue(s')$.

\emph{\ref{E:coll}.} \ref{H:coll} makes the members a completion requires issue the set in
$\Hpre$, and \ref{G:coll} orders their calls first. Unknown names, and root shapes without a
recorded root, require every member.

\emph{\ref{E:probe}.} Let $p$ complete in $\Hpre$. By \ref{H:probe} and \ref{G:probe}, $s_p$ is
issued before $\compl(p)$. By (O3), $\Malt^{-1}(s_p)$ is not posted before $p$, and a later
receive of the rank is posted after $\compl(p)$, so $s_p$ is unmatched at $\compl(p)$. A receive with $p$'s envelope posted then would take $s_p$: by (O3)
the earlier sends of the channel that $p$ accepts are matched to receives posted before $p$,
and the receives before $p$ that accept $s_p$ have $\Malt$-sends, issued as \ref{H:pending} keeps
$p$; \ref{G:probe} orders all these matches before $\compl(p)$.

$X$ is finite and no rule asks for more: a posted receive whose $\Malt$-send is not issued in
$\Hpre$, or that $\Malt$ leaves unmatched, is simply not matched in $X$.
\end{proof}

The proof covers the generic check when it accepts, and the fast check for a steal at a blocking
receive. There \ref{H:changed} and (O1) leave $\compl(s)$ as the only possible successor of
$\mnode(r,s)$; $\Graph$ without $\mnode(r,s)$ and without the \ref{G:ready} edge of a ready $s$
is a subgraph of $\hat G_\Bt$, which the check requires acyclic; and the check tests the two
remaining paths, from $\compl(s)$ to a predecessor of $\mnode(r,s)$ and from $\issue(s)$ to
$\issue(r)$. Obligation (O3) is needed because the graph has order edges into $\compl(p)$ only
for messages that $\Malt$ matches. Without it, the theorem fails: rank~0 runs
$\mpicall{Irecv}(r,\ANY)$, $\mpicall{Probe}(p,\text{from }2)$, $\mpicall{Wait}(r)$,
$\mpicall{Recv}(r_2,\text{from }2)$; rank~1 sends $s_o$, rank~2 sends $s$; in $\trace$
$r\gets s_o$, $p$ reports $s$, $r_2\gets s$, and $\Bobs=0$. The graph of the steal $r\mapsto s$
is acyclic at every $\Bt$, but $r$ takes $s$ before $p$ can report it. A probe of rank~1
reporting a later message of rank~1 fails alike, on the released $s_o$. (O3) rejects both; (H8)
of \cref{app:mono} would cut them instead.

\subsection{Monotonicity}
\label{app:mono}

\paragraph{A nonblocking changed receive.}
\Cref{thm:mono} is restricted to blocking receives because it fails for a nonblocking one.
Rank~0 runs $\mpicall{Irecv}(r,\ANY)$, $\mpicall{Irecv}(r',\text{from }1)$,
$\mpicall{Wait}(r)$, $\mpicall{Wait}(r')$, $\mpicall{Recv}(r_3,\ANY)$; rank~1 sends $s_o$ then
$s_1$, rank~2 sends $s$, all to rank~0 in standard mode with one tag. In $\trace$, $r\gets s_o$,
$r'\gets s_1$, $r_3\gets s$, and $\Bobs=0$. Take the kernel steal $r\mapsto s$. At $\Bt=0$,
\ref{H:send} cuts rank~1 at $s_o$, which lost its receive; $s_1$ is not issued, $r'$ is not
judged, and the steal is feasible. At $\Bt=\infty$, $r'$ is judged and (O1) fails for
$(r',s_1)$, as $r'$ accepts the released $s_o$. Checking the obligations of every posted
receive, judged or not, would reject at $\Bt=0$ too, but a variant defeats that: between $r$
and $r'$, rank~0 sends in rendezvous to a rank~3 that receives it only after a message that
rank~1 sends after $s_o$; below both sizes rank~0 stops at that send and never posts $r'$. The
natural argument, that newly judged receives are unchanged pairs whose obligations held in
$\trace$, fails here: their obligations mention the released $s_o$.

Let $U(r)$ be the receives and probes of $\mathit{rank}(r)$ posted after $r$ and before
$\compl(r)$, or after $r$ if $r$ does not complete in $\trace$; $U(r)=\emptyset$ if $r$ blocks.

\begin{theorem}[Monotonicity, in full]\label{thm:app-mono}
Let $\Malt$ be a kernel steal $r\mapsto s$, $\Bobs\le\Bt\le\Bt'$, and $\Malt$ feasible at
$\Bt$. Then (a) $\Hpre(\Bt)\subseteq\Hpre(\Bt')$ and $\judged(\Bt)\subseteq\judged(\Bt')$;
(b) $\Malt$ survives at $\Bt'$ and $\Graph(\Bt')$ is acyclic; (c) an obligation fails at
$\Bt'$ only for a member of $U(r)$ that accepts $s_o$ or a probe of $U(r)$ that reports $s$;
(d) hence $\Malt$ is feasible at $\Bt'$ if $r$ is blocking or $U(r)$ has no such member;
(e) the thresholds $\Bt\ge\Bobs$ at which $\Malt$ is feasible form an interval.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) Only \ref{H:send} mentions $\Bt$, and $\blocks_{\Bt'}(x)$ implies $\blocks_\Bt(x)$; so
$F_\Bt(X)\subseteq F_{\Bt'}(X)$ for all $X$, and by induction with monotonicity
$\Hpre(\Bt)=F_\Bt^m(\Hpre(\Bt))\subseteq F_{\Bt'}^m(\trace)=\Hpre(\Bt')$ for large $m$. Postings
and issues only grow, and so does $\judged$.

(b) $r$, $s$, and the completion of a blocking $r$ stay in $\Hpre(\Bt')$, so $\Malt$ survives.
Call the nodes of $\Graph(\Bt)$ old. (i) An edge of $\Graph(\Bt')$ between old nodes is one of
$\Graph(\Bt)$: edges depend on $\Malt$, on the order of $\trace$ and on $\blocks$, which drops
rendezvous edges as $\Bt$ grows. (ii) Every predecessor of an old node is old. Program order
stays on its rank. Edges into $\compl(r')$, into $\compl(s')$ from a match, into a ready send,
into a collective's completion and from $\issue(s_p)$ start at old nodes by \ref{H:recv},
\ref{H:send}, \ref{H:ready}, \ref{H:coll} and \ref{H:probe} at $\Bt$; a match's calls are old
by the definition of $\judged$. An order edge into an old $\mnode(r',s')$, or an old $\compl(p)$
with $s'=s_p$, starts at the match of a receive $q$. On the receiver side, $q$ is posted before
$r'$ and its $\Malt$-send is issued in $\Hpre(\Bt)$, else \ref{H:pending} removes $r'$. On the
sender side, $q=\Malt^{-1}(s'')$ with $s''$ before $s'$ and $r'\accepts s''$; $s''$ is issued,
and we show that $q$ is posted before $r'$. If both pairs belong to $\Mobs$, a later $q$ would
close a cycle through $\mnode(r',s')$ and $\mnode(q,s'')$ in $\hat G_{\Bt'}$ by \ref{G:order}
and \ref{G:fifo} (for a probe, through $\compl(p)$ and program order), against (F2). If
$(q,s'')=(r,s)$ and $r'$ precedes $r$, then $s$ precedes $\Mobs(r')$ and is taken in $\trace$ by
$r_2$, after $r$, which gives the same cycle, or by nobody, against (F1). If $(r',s')=(r,s)$, the
kernel property gives it, as $s''\ne s_o$. The released $s_o$ has no receive and starts no
edge. (iii) A new match node belongs to a
receive judged at $\Bt'$ but not at $\Bt$, neither $r$ (judged at $\Bt$) nor $r_2$ (no match),
so it is a pair of $\Mobs$, and the edges between new nodes are edges of $\hat G_{\Bt'}$. A cycle
through an old node is old throughout by (ii), so it lies in $\Graph(\Bt)$ by (i); a cycle of
new nodes lies in $\hat G_{\Bt'}$ by (iii). Neither exists.

(c) Obligations do not depend on $\Bt$, so those at $\Bt$ still hold. Take
$r'\in\judged(\Bt')\setminus\judged(\Bt)$; its pair $(r',s')$ belongs to $\Mobs$. For (O1), let
$s''$ precede $s'$ with $r'\accepts s''$. By (F1), $q=\Mobs^{-1}(s'')$ is posted before $r'$,
and $\Malt^{-1}(s'')=q$ unless $s''\in\{s,s_o\}$. If $s''=s$, then $q=r_2$, and $r$ precedes
$r_2$, hence $r'$. If $s''=s_o$, then $q=r$, and (O1) fails for a receive after $r$ on its rank
that accepts $s_o$. For (O2), let $q$ precede $r'$ with $q\accepts s'$; by (F1) $\Mobs(q)$ is
defined, and $\Malt(q)$ is too unless $q=r_2$, which \ref{H:pending} excludes. New probes are
alike, with one more case: (O3) fails if $q=r$ and $s_p=s$. A failing receive or probe lies on
$\mathit{rank}(r)$ after $r$ and before the cut of \ref{H:changed}: in $U(r)$.

(d) follows from (b) and (c). (e) Let $\Bobs\le\Bt_1\le\Bt_2\le\Bt_3$ with $\Malt$ feasible at
$\Bt_1$ and $\Bt_3$. By (b) for $(\Bt_1,\Bt_2)$, $\Malt$ survives at $\Bt_2$ with an acyclic
graph; by (a) for $(\Bt_2,\Bt_3)$, every obligation at $\Bt_2$ is one at $\Bt_3$, where it holds.
\end{proof}

\emph{Swaps.} Step (iii) needs every newly judged pair to belong to $\Mobs$, and step (ii) uses
the kernel property of the single changed pair. A swap's second pair $(r_2,s_o)$ can be judged
first at $\Bt'$ and has neither property, so monotonicity for swaps is open; \tool profiles
them at every breakpoint.

\emph{A rule that restores monotonicity.}
(H8) \emph{A receive or probe is removed if a send it accepts that precedes its $\Malt$-send, or
report, on the channel is not matched by $\Malt$ to a receive posted before it; a probe is also
removed if $\Malt$ matches its report to a receive posted before it.} Its condition depends only
on $\Malt$ and the order of $\trace$, so \cref{lem:app-fix} and (a), (b) keep their proofs, the
failing cases of (c) are cut instead of rejected, and (d) holds for every kernel steal. (H8)
also cuts the witness of \cref{app:sound}, and \cref{thm:sound} survives the extra rule, as its
proof uses the rules only as closure properties. \tool does not implement (H8): it rejects these
cases by (O1)--(O3) and judges a steal at a nonblocking receive at every breakpoint.

\emph{Alternatives that change no match.}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:app-nochange}
Let a probe or completion-order alternative be feasible at $\Bt\ge\Bobs$. Then it is feasible at
every $\Bt'\ge\Bt$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be the changed record. \ref{H:changed} cuts its rank right after $v$, and a completion
node has no successor but the next call of its rank, so $\evret(v)$ is a sink of $\Graph$.
Without the edges into it, $\Graph(\Bt')$ has only pairs of $\Mobs$, and every other probe
reports as in $\trace$: it is a subgraph of $\hat G_{\Bt'}$, which (F2) makes acyclic. The
proof of \cref{thm:app-mono}(a) uses only that \ref{H:send} is antitone in $\Bt$, so
$\Hpre(\Bt)\subseteq\Hpre(\Bt')$, and $v$ survives at $\Bt'$. The obligations of the receives
and of the other probes are those of $\Mobs$, which hold by (F1), and those of $v$ do not depend
on $\Bt$.
\end{proof}

\noindent The proof also shows that above $\Bobs$ such an alternative is feasible iff it
survives and, if $v$ is a probe, $v$ meets (O3). The fast checks of these alternatives build
no graph for this reason, and leave a threshold at which $\hat G_\Bt$ is cyclic to the generic
check.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:obsmono}]
With $\Changed=\emptyset$, (a) gives $\Hpre(\Mobs,\Bt')\supseteq\Hpre(\Mobs,\Bt)=\trace$;
obligations do not depend on the threshold; $\Graph(\Bt')$ is $\Graph(\Bt)$ minus some
rendezvous edges.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Deadlocks are not monotone}
\label{app:nonmono}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{prop:nonmono}]
\emph{Not upward-closed.} Two ranks each send 400 bytes to the other, then receive. For
$\Bt<400$ each send waits for a receive posted after the other send returns, so every execution
that cannot be extended deadlocks; for $\Bt\ge400$ the run terminates.
\emph{Not downward-closed.} Rank~0 runs $\mpicall{Recv}(a,\ANY)$, $\mpicall{Send}(w\to1)$,
$\mpicall{Recv}(d,\text{from }2)$; rank~1 runs $\mpicall{Recv}(x,\text{from }0)$,
$\mpicall{Recv}(v,\text{from }2)$; rank~2 sends $u$ to rank~1, then $s_2$ to rank~0; rank~3
sends $s_3$ to rank~0; all messages have 4 bytes. In $\trace$, $a\gets s_3$, $x\gets w$,
$v\gets u$, $d\gets s_2$, and $\Bobs=0$. Take the steal $a\mapsto s_2$. For $\Bt<4$, $s_2$
follows the return of $u$, which follows $v$, posted after $x$ completes, which needs $w$,
issued after $a$ completes: no execution has $a\gets s_2$, and the check rejects, as
\ref{H:send} cuts rank~2 at $u$. For $\Bt\ge4$ the steal is feasible, and in every execution of
$\SP(\trace)$ with $a\gets s_2$, $d$ never completes, since rank~2 sends nothing more. The
stolen message must be buffered, as in the \MBI loop races.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cost}
\label{app:cost}

Let $T$ bound the tags on any channel read by an \ANY-tag receive or probe, and $\pi$ count the
probes that report a message.

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:cost}]
(i) The enumeration scans, for each wildcard receive $r$ and each of the at most $|\Ranks|$
sources sending to its rank, that source's queue up to the first send it does not skip, and
emits at most one steal there. A skipped send is unavailable or taken by a receive posted before
$r$, and yields swaps only. A swap pairs $r$ with a wildcard receive of its rank: one branch
tests it, and in the other the partner accepts $s$ and $\Mobs(r)$, which come from different
sources by (F1). So there are at most $\sum_q\binom{w_q}{2}$ distinct swaps; the list may name a
swap once from each of its receives.
(ii) The index of \ref{H:pending} has at most $4(|\Malt|+\pi)$ entries, sorted once. A record
leaves the prefix at most twice (return, then call), and each removal re-evaluates $O(1)$
instances, \ref{H:pending} by one bisection, plus \ref{H:probe} per probe of the removed send
and \ref{H:coll} for one set. With a count of issuing members per set this is
$O((n+|\Malt|+\pi)\log n)$; the implementation rescans a set per removed member, adding
$O(\sum_C|C|^2)\le O(|\Ranks|\,n)$. (O2) uses the earliest unmatched receive per class, (O1) one
bisection per tag (\cref{lem:app-chain}). The graph has $O(n)$ program-order and hub edges and,
per pair or probe, a match node and at most $4+T$ order edges, each found by a bisection; cycle
detection is linear.
(iii) A steal at a blocking receive, and a probe or completion-order alternative, is judged at
$\Bobs$ and $\infty$ and, if the two differ, by bisection over the $\mu$ sizes: $O(\log \mu)$
checks, exact by \cref{thm:app-mono}(d) and \cref{lem:app-nochange}. A steal at a nonblocking
receive and a swap are judged at all $\mu+2$ breakpoints.
\end{proof}

By \cref{thm:app-mono}(e), the bisection of the profiler is exact when a
steal is feasible at $\Bobs$ or at $\infty$. Infeasible at both, it may miss a feasible middle
interval, which (d) excludes for a blocking $r$.

\subsection{Completeness}
\label{app:complete}

Completeness must be relative to the horizon. In the first witness of \cref{app:mono} at
$\Bt=\infty$, rank~0 posting $r$ and $r'$, rank~2 sending $s$, $r$ taking it and
$\mpicall{Wait}(r)$ returning is a matching-preserving reordering of a prefix of $\trace$ for
$r\mapsto s$, yet the check rejects. The horizon rules keep such a reordering inside $\Hpre$,
but the cycle or failed obligation can lie in the part of $\Hpre$ the reordering omits.

\begin{definition}\label{def:app-real}
Let $\Malt$ survive at $\Bt$ with horizon $\Hpre$. A \emph{realisation} of $\Malt$ over $\Hpre$
is an execution of $\SP(\trace)$ at $\Bt$ whose events are the calls and returns of $\Hpre$ and
$\mnode(r,\Malt(r))$ for each $r\in\judged$ that $\Malt$ matches, and in which each probe
reports $s_p$. For a kernel steal, it is a matching-preserving reordering of the whole horizon.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\Cref{thm:complete}]\label{thm:app-complete}
Assume (C1) every collective of $\trace$ has a known shape and, if the shape has a root, a
recorded root; (C2) the ready-send clause of \ref{E:compl} requires the receive that the send is
\emph{matched to} to be posted, as the standard's ready mode does. Let $\Malt$ survive at $\Bt$.
Then $\Malt$ is feasible at $\Bt$ iff a realisation of $\Malt$ over $\Hpre(\trace,\Malt,\Bt)$
exists.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
($\Rightarrow$) $\SP(\trace)\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre)$, and the execution built for
\cref{thm:sound} is a realisation. ($\Leftarrow$) Let $X$ be a realisation; its matches are
pairs of $\Malt$. (O1): let $r\in\judged$, $s=\Malt(r)$, $s'$ before $s$ with $r\accepts s'$.
Then $s'$ is issued and, by \ref{E:fifo}, matched before $\mnode(r,s)$ to $q=\Malt^{-1}(s')$; if
$q$ were posted after $r$, \ref{E:order} would put $\mnode(r,s)$ before $\mnode(q,s')$. (O2): by
\ref{E:order}, every receive before $r$ that accepts $s$ is matched in $X$, hence by $\Malt$.
(O3): by \ref{E:probe}, $s_p$ is unmatched at $\compl(p)$ and a receive posted then would take
it; by \ref{E:fifo} the earlier sends of the channel that $p$ accepts are matched before
$\compl(p)$, to receives posted before $p$, and by \ref{E:order} so are the receives before $p$
that accept $s_p$, to sends other than $s_p$. Acyclicity: $X$ respects \ref{G:po} by
\ref{E:po}, \ref{G:match} by \ref{E:match} and \ref{E:compl}, \ref{G:fifo} by \ref{E:fifo},
\ref{G:order} by \ref{E:order}, \ref{G:ready} by \ref{E:compl} and (C2), \ref{G:coll} by
\ref{E:coll} and (C1), and \ref{G:probe} by \ref{E:probe}. $X$ contains every node of $\Graph$,
so its order is topological.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Necessity of the horizon rules.}
\ref{H:recv}--\ref{H:probe} and \ref{H:ready} remove only events that no execution with the
matches of $\Malt$, the reports $s_p$ and the completion records of $\trace$ can contain, given
its calls (\ref{H:recv} and \ref{H:send} by \ref{E:compl}, \ref{H:coll} by \ref{E:coll} under
(C1), \ref{H:probe} by \ref{E:probe}, \ref{H:ready} by \ref{E:compl} under (C2)).
\ref{H:changed} defines what \Lone claims. Its cut right after the posting of a changed receive
$r$ that never completes in $\trace$ is needed for soundness: if a receive posted later on the
rank is judged and $r$ accepts its message, \ref{G:order} puts $\mnode(r,\Malt(r))$ before it,
and a test of $r$'s request after that receive, which failed in $\trace$, succeeds.
\ref{H:pending} is necessary for the match of a
deprived receive $r''$, not for its posting. Example: rank~0 runs
$\mpicall{Irecv}(q,\text{from }1,\text{any tag})$,
$\mpicall{Irecv}(r'',\text{from }1,\text{tag }5)$, $\mpicall{Recv}(r,\ANY)$, then sends to
rank~1, which then sends $t$ and $u$ (tag 5); in $\trace$, $q\gets t$, $r''\gets u$, and $r$
takes a message of rank~3. A steal of a message of rank~2 at $r$ never survives, as
\ref{H:pending} removes $r''$ and $r$, yet $r$ can take it while $q$ and $r''$ stay pending. A
weaker rule would remove only the record that completes $r''$, treat $r''$ as pending, and also
remove the receives that would take a later message of the channel of $\Malt(r'')$. A deprived
receive then stays pending inside the horizon, so the rule needs its own forms of (O2) and of
\cref{thm:sound}. \tool does not use it, and whether it makes the horizon the largest prefix over
which $\Malt$ is realisable is open.

\paragraph{Where the implementation is more conservative.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item The fast check, used for steals at blocking receives, decides reachability from
  $\compl(s)$ in $\hat G_\Bt$, which also has the pairs $(r,s_o)$, $(r_2,s)$ and the events beyond
  $\Hpre$, so it can reject where the generic check accepts. On traces of up to $2\cdot10^5$
  records the generic check confirms each such rejection by a cycle; above that, the report
  counts the rejections left unconfirmed.
\item Unknown collectives and root shapes without a recorded root are barriers, a case (C1)
  excludes.
\item Outside the model, a receive freed while pending counts as unmatched, though it completed
  unobserved, and an \mpicall{Mrecv} is ordered from its own posting, though its message was
  fixed at its \mpicall{Mprobe}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Deadlocks inside the horizon}
\label{app:l1dead}

Let $\Malt$ be feasible at $\Bt$ with horizon $\Hpre$, and $X$ an execution built for
\cref{thm:sound}. If rank $i$ has records beyond $\Hpre$, $\kappa_i$ is its first record whose
return is not in $\Hpre$. A rank \emph{terminates in $\Hpre$} if all its records, the last a
finalize, are in $\Hpre$.

\begin{definition}[Contained deadlock, in full]\label{def:app-dead}
$D$ is a contained deadlock if every $i\in D$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(D\arabic*)]
\item $i$ is not the rank of a changed receive that completes in $\Hpre$, nor of a changed
  record;
\item $\kappa_i$ is a blocking send, receive, probe or collective, or a wait over a request set
  $Q_i$ (not a test or iprobe). $W_i$ is $\{$the operation of $\kappa_i\}$, or $Q_i$ for a
  wait-any or wait-some, or $\{e\}$ for one $e\in Q_i$ for a wait or wait-all;
\item no $e\in W_i$ has a partner in $X$: a receive or probe $e$ is unmatched in $X$ and accepts
  no send of $\Hpre$ that $X$ leaves unmatched; a send $e$ blocks at $\Bt$, is unmatched, and no
  receive of $\Hpre$ that $X$ leaves unmatched accepts it; for a collective, a member its shape
  requires does not issue the set in $\Hpre$;
\item each rank that could later supply a partner of $e\in W_i$ is in $D$ or terminates in
  $\Hpre$: the source of a receive or probe (all ranks of the communicator for \ANY), the
  destination of a send, and for a collective one member missing in (D3) whose $\kappa_j$, if it
  is in $D$, is not its call of the set. For $j\in D$, the operation of $\kappa_j$ is not a
  partner of $e$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\Cref{thm:l1dead}]\label{thm:app-dead}
Let $D$ be a contained deadlock and $\Hpre^+=\Hpre\cup\{\evcall(\kappa_i)\mid i\in D\}$. For
every $\prog\in\Cons(\trace,\Hpre^+)$, every execution $X$ of $\prog$ given by
\cref{thm:sound} and every execution $X'$ of $\prog$ at $\Bt$ that extends $X$, no $i\in D$
returns from $\kappa_i$ in $X'$, and no operation of $W_i$ completes in $X'$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By (D1) and the proof of \cref{thm:sound}, the records of $\Hpre$ on a rank of $D$ return their
results in $\trace$, so by consistency on $\Hpre^+$ the next call of rank $i\in D$ is $\kappa_i$.
Let $v$ be the first event of $X'$ after $X$ that completes an operation of some $W_i$,
$i\in D$. Rank $i$ makes no call between $\kappa_i$ and its return, so $v$ is that return; and
every return of a $\kappa_j$ completes an operation of $W_j$. So before $v$ no rank of $D$ has
returned from its $\kappa_j$ or, by \ref{E:po}, called anything after it. The operation $e$
completed at $v$ needs a partner before $v$: a matched send or receive (\ref{E:compl}), an
issued unmatched send (\ref{E:probe}), or the required calls (\ref{E:coll}). A partner in $X$ is
excluded by (D3), as nothing is matched twice (\ref{E:match}). A partner issued after $X$
belongs by (D4) to a terminated rank, which by (P2) calls nothing, or to a $j\in D$; then it is
$\kappa_j$, excluded by (D4), or a later call, which needs the return of $\kappa_j$ before $v$.
\end{proof}

Consistency on $\Hpre$ alone would not do: when $\kappa_i$ is not issued in $\Hpre$ (after
\ref{H:recv}, \ref{H:coll}, \ref{H:pending} or \ref{H:ready}), a program consistent on $\Hpre$
may issue in its place a send that completes another rank's blocked receive.
The implementation skips a test or an iprobe at the cut, judges a \mpicall{Waitany} or
\mpicall{Waitsome} by all of $Q_i$, and for a wait or wait-all requires every blocked request's
partner ranks in $D$ or terminated, which is stronger than (D3)--(D4). It checks partner ranks
rather than whether $\kappa_j$ is a partner; its tests for a present message or receive cover
that, except for a ready send removed by \ref{H:ready}, and we know no trace on which that
matters.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:app-deadrank}
Let $\Malt$ be feasible at $\Bt\ge\Bobs$ and $D$ a contained deadlock. Then $D$ contains the
rank of a receive on which $\Malt$ and $\Mobs$ differ. For a steal or a swap at $r$, that rank is
$\mathit{rank}(r)$, no changed receive completes in $\Hpre$, and $r$ is nonblocking.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Ranks'$ be the ranks of the receives on which $\Malt$ and $\Mobs$ differ, and suppose
$D\cap \Ranks'=\emptyset$. No rank of $\Ranks'$ terminates in $\Hpre$: a changed receive cuts its rank by
\ref{H:changed}, and a receive that $\Mobs$ matches and $\Malt$ does not loses its completing
record by \ref{H:recv}. By (D4), then, no partner of an operation of a $W_i$, $i\in D$, lies on a
rank of $\Ranks'$, so $\Malt$ and $\Mobs$ agree on these operations: a receive of $W_i$ lies outside
$\Ranks'$, and a send whose $\Malt$- and $\Mobs$-receives differed would have its destination in
$\Ranks'$. By (D1), no probe of $W_i$ is changed. For $j\in D$, (K) an operation of rank $j$ that is
not issued in $\Hpre$ and is not the operation of $\kappa_j$ belongs to a record after $\kappa_j$,
so \ref{G:po} orders $\evret(\kappa_j)$ before its issue.

Let $i\in D$. In $\trace$, $\kappa_i$ returns and completes some $e\in W_i$ (for a wait-any or
wait-some, one it returned), so $\compl(e)=\evret(\kappa_i)$. We find $j\in D$ with a path from
$\evret(\kappa_j)$ to $\evret(\kappa_i)$ in $\hat G_\Bt$.
\emph{A receive or probe $e$.} Let $s_e$ be its send in $\trace$, $\Mobs(e)$ or the report. If
$s_e$ were issued in $\Hpre$, it would be a partner of $e$ in $X$, against (D3): if $e$ is a
receive posted in $\Hpre$, it is judged and matched to $s_e$; otherwise $X$ leaves $s_e$
unmatched, since $\Mobs^{-1}(s_e)$ is $e$, or for a probe a receive posted after it by (F1), and
neither is posted in $\Hpre$. So $s_e$ is not issued in $\Hpre$, its rank $j$ does not terminate
in $\Hpre$, and by (D4) $j\in D$ and $s_e$ is not the operation of $\kappa_j$. By (K),
\ref{G:match} and \ref{G:probe}, $\evret(\kappa_j)$ precedes $\issue(s_e)$, which precedes
$\compl(e)$.
\emph{A send $e$.} It blocks at $\Bt$ by (D3) and completes in $\trace$. Since
$\Hpre(\trace,\Mobs,\Bt)=\trace$ for $\Bt\ge\Bobs$ (proof of \cref{lem:obsmono}), \ref{H:send}
gives it a receive $q_e=\Mobs^{-1}(e)=\Malt^{-1}(e)$. If $q_e$ were posted in $\Hpre$, it would be a partner
of $e$ in $X$: judged and matched to $e$ if $e$ is issued there, else a receive of $\Hpre$ that
$X$ leaves unmatched and that accepts $e$. So, as before, its rank $j$ is in $D$, and
$\evret(\kappa_j)$ precedes $\issue(q_e)\to\mnode(q_e,e)\to\compl(e)$, the last edge by
\ref{G:match}.
\emph{A collective $e$.} (D4) gives a required member $j$ that does not issue the set in
$\Hpre$ and is in $D$ or terminates in $\Hpre$. It cannot terminate there: it issues the set in
$\trace$, by \ref{H:coll} for $\Mobs$, as $e$ completes there. So $j\in D$, its call of the set is
not $\kappa_j$, and by (K) and \ref{G:coll} $\evret(\kappa_j)$ precedes that call, which precedes
$\compl(e)$.

Following such $j$ from any rank of the finite $D$ closes a cycle in $\hat G_\Bt$, against (F2).
So $D\cap \Ranks'\ne\emptyset$. For a steal or a swap at $r$, the receives on which $\Malt$ and
$\Mobs$ differ are $r$, its partner or $r_2$, all of $\mathit{rank}(r)$, so
$\mathit{rank}(r)\in D$, and (D1) leaves no changed receive completing in $\Hpre$. A blocking
$r$ would complete there: survival keeps its call and its message, and only \ref{H:send} removes
a return.
\end{proof}

\noindent \tool therefore looks for a contained deadlock only after a nonblocking changed
receive, and at thresholds of at least $\Bobs$. The bound is needed: below $\Bobs$, a send of
$\trace$ that no receive accepts, that blocks at $\Bt$ and whose destination terminates in
$\Hpre$ is a contained deadlock of its own rank under any alternative that leaves both ranks
alone.

\subsection{Single-path is unsound}
\label{app:sp}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{lem:sp}]
Rank~0 runs $\mpicall{Recv}(a,\ANY)$, a reply, $\mpicall{Recv}(b,\ANY)$, a reply; ranks~1 and~2
each send a result $s_w$ to rank~0, then post $\mpicall{Recv}(x_w,\text{from }0)$. In the
recorded run $a\gets s_1$, the first reply $y_1$ goes to rank~1, $b\gets s_2$, and the second
reply goes to rank~2. Let $\prog_1=\SP(\trace)$, and let $\prog_2$ reply to the source of the
status just received; both are consistent with all of $\trace$. Take $\Bt=0$, where every send
returns only after its match, and the steal $a\mapsto s_2$.
$\prog_1$: let an execution match $a$ to $s_2$. Rank~0 then sends $y_1$, which only $x_1$
accepts, and rank~1 posts $x_1$ after $s_1$ returns. The receives of rank~0 that accept $s_1$
are $a$, already matched, and $b$, posted after $y_1$ returns. So each of $y_1$, $s_1$ returns
only after the other: neither does. An execution that cannot be extended has issued both, as
each could otherwise be appended, so it deadlocks.
$\prog_2$: $s_2$ and $a$ are issued, matched and return; rank~0 replies $z_2$ to rank~2, which
posts $x_2$; they match and return; rank~0 posts $b$ and rank~1 issues $s_1$; they match and
return; rank~0 replies $z_1$ to rank~1, which posts $x_1$; they match and return. Each match
takes the first message of its channel into the first receive of its rank that accepts it, so
\ref{E:po}--\ref{E:probe} hold, and the execution realises the steal and terminates.
\end{proof}

The \Lone check accepts this steal at $\Bt=0$ (horizon: rank~0 up to $a$, the call of the
released $s_1$, and $s_2$), and $\{1\}$ is no contained deadlock, as rank~0 is cut by
\ref{H:changed}.
