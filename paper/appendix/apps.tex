\section{Further results}
\label{app:apps}

\paragraph{Happens-before candidates (\cref{sec:graph}).} On our \nHBTraces corpus and
application traces, placing receives at their posting misses \nHBPostMissed pairs the check
accepts, among them \nHBMGPostMissed alternatives of NPB MG's documented bug (RQ6), and all
\nHBReplayStrettoOnly we replayed were realised by runs of the program. Placing them at their
completion selected no impossible pair there, only on the example of \cref{sec:graph}
(idiom~14) and on \nHBSynCompOnlyNotRealised of \nHBSynPrograms random programs.

\paragraph{Sampled replays (RQ1).} On AMG, a random sample of \nAMGsample of its alternatives,
which claim no failure, was replayed: all \nAMGsampleMatched matched their pins,
\nAMGsampleFinish finished, and so did \nAMGsampleControls controls. On Nekbone, whose late
alternatives pin tens of thousands of receives each, \nNeksampleMatched of \nNeksample matched,
\nNeksampleFinish finished, and \nNeksampleControls controls finished.

\paragraph{HYPRE~2.10.1 and 2.11.0 (RQ6).} The trace tells a cross alternative from a same
one. An exchange sends its requests, counts the replies to expect with an \mpicall{Allreduce},
and only then probes. A request and a probe therefore belong to one exchange exactly when the
probing rank has made one more collective call before the probe than the sending rank before
the request. The same alternatives of 2.10.1 finish with the residual of their control: all
\nHypreFourLocalAlts on four processes, and \nHypreEightLocalSameResidual of the
\nHypreEightLocalAlts on eight; those of 2.11.0 finish too, \nHypreFixedFourLocalFinish of
\nHypreFixedFourLocalAlts on four processes and \nHypreFixedEightLocalFinish of
\nHypreFixedEightLocalAlts on eight. The other \nHypreFixedEightLocalOther replays and
\nHypreFixedEightLocalControlsFailed controls of 2.11.0 on eight processes, and
\nHypreEightLocalOther replay of 2.10.1, exited with status~15 without finishing; plain runs
launched the same way never did, their output was not kept, and we count them as neither
hanging nor finishing. NINJA hangs 2.10.1 in \nNinjaHypreEightAppHangSetup of
\nNinjaHypreEightAppRuns runs on eight processes in its application-centric mode and in
\nNinjaHypreEightRandomShortHangSetup of \nNinjaHypreEightRandomShortRuns with 10\% of sends
delayed by 50\,ms; on four processes only random noise does
(\nNinjaHypreFourRandomShortHangSetup of \nNinjaHypreFourRandomShortRuns). A trace has a cross alternative only if its order lets a neighbour reach
the next exchange while this rank probes. The run finishes either way: the order is common, the
hang it allows is not. We did not measure the threshold of MPICH, under which every forced cross
match happened. In a trace on four processes the probe alternatives fall at four call sites:
HYPRE's own \mpicall{Iprobe} wrapper in its data exchange, the diagonal-scaling exchange,
ParaSails's \texttt{ReceiveReplyPrunedRows}, and its \texttt{ReceiveRequest}. Every alternative
replayed in the campaign lies at the last, the probe for requests (\texttt{ParaSails.c:166}).

\paragraph{HYPRE~3.2.0 (RQ6).} PETSc's \texttt{ex2} runs with \texttt{-pc\_type hypre
-pc\_hypre\_type parasails -pc\_hypre\_parasails\_nlevels 2 -pc\_hypre\_parasails\_thresh 0.01}.
PETSc~3.26.0 passes the level count to HYPRE only together with the threshold, a separate bug:
with the level count alone, all \nPetscNoThreshSameAsOne analysed traces of a first pass have
the findings of a one-level trace, and none has a cross alternative. hypre's own drivers fix the
level count at one; \texttt{ex5} gets an option that sets it and nothing else. The scan covers
the thresholds 0.01, 0.05 and 0.1 (PETSc's default), one to three levels and both symmetry
settings, on \texttt{ex2} and on \texttt{ex45}'s 3D Laplacian, at four processes and in part at
eight. Over the scan, the cross alternatives are realisable from thresholds between
\BPetscScanMin and \BPetscScanMax bytes, and on \texttt{ex45} some at every threshold. Untraced, all
\nHypreNewStressRuns runs of \texttt{ex5} at one and two levels and all \nPetscStressRuns of
\texttt{ex2} finished. NINJA's system-centric mode hangs \texttt{ex5} in
\nNinjaHypreNewFourSystemHang of \nNinjaHypreNewFourSystemRuns runs on four processes and
\nNinjaHypreNewEightSystemHang of \nNinjaHypreNewEightSystemRuns on eight, and \texttt{ex2} in none; its application-centric mode ends every run of \texttt{ex2} with exit
status~\NinjaPetscAppStatus after its learning run. The untraced runs and the runs of the fixed
variant shared the machine with the PETSc replays.

\paragraph{NPB MG~2.4.1 (RQ6).} \nMGControls controls finished, \nMGControlsSame of them with the
residual of the traced run. Random delays change the residual in \nMGStressChanged of
\nMGStressRuns untraced runs of version~2.4.1 on \nMGStressRanks processes, half of them
delayed, and NINJA in \nNinjaMGSixteenRandomChanged (random noise) and
\nNinjaMGSixteenAppChanged (application-centric) of \nNinjaMGSixteenRandomRuns runs on 16.

\paragraph{deal.II~9.3.0-rc1 (RQ6).} The \nDealTraced clean runs of \texttt{mapping\_01} use
\nDealRanks processes and predict \nDealAlts probe alternatives that take the next call's
message. Forced, all \nDealReplayed finish with status~0 and print wrong values in the two
consecutive calls the code predicts (\nDealMatched report every pin matched); \nDealControlsSame
of \nDealControls controls print the expected output. \nDealStressDelayChanged of
\nDealStressDelayRuns runs with random delays print wrong values.

\paragraph{Wildcard-heavy correct code (RQ6).} AMG's solver exchanges its matrix data with
probes on any source. One traced run on eight processes, with \nAMGsends messages, \nAMGwild
wildcard receives and \nAMGprobes probes, gives \nAMGalts probe alternatives and \nAMGrecvAlts
same-shape receive alternatives. All are warnings or information, none a contained deadlock.
RQ1's sample finished under every pin. In Nekbone, every finding is a \cls{1f} at the
binary-tree reduction \texttt{gp2}, whose inner nodes take their children's contributions with
one tag from any source. Only the communication suite's timing test calls it; the solver sums
with \mpicall{Allreduce}. The check accepts \nNekAlts alternatives there, the same four at each
of the test's calls, and rejects \nNekRejected for one reason: the other sender's next message
with that tag is sent only after the receive completes, so no execution delivers it there. Its
RQ1 sample finished under every pin too.
